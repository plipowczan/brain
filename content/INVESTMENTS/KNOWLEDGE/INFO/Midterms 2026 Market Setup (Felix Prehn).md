# Midterms 2026 Market Setup (Felix Prehn)

## 🗒️ Description

A 20-minute YouTube video by Felix Nikolas Prehn (economist, former investment banker, founder of The Prehn Institute), published five days before the US midterms of 3 November 2026, plus its 13-page PDF "companion briefing". The PDF follows the video's script section by section and says it adds no claims of its own. What it does add is the numbers in one place, a source list and a 5-question checklist. I merged both into this note.

The thesis: the S&P 500 looks calm, but most US stocks have already been through a bear market. At the same time three forces are pushing new money in the same direction, right at a midterm. Prehn says he has never seen all three line up in his career, and he quotes Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson calling it one of the best setups of his career.

🚀 **TL;DR**

- **A green index over a red market.** About 6 in 10 US stocks are down more than 20% from their highs. Among chip makers it is 9 in 10, and 4 in 10 have lost half their value. The index holds up because ~8 giant tech names now make up ~40% of it, against ~26% for the top 10 at the dot-com peak.
- **That is closer to the bottom than the top.** A bubble top is when everything rises together and nobody is worried. Here most of the pain has already been taken.
- **Three forces at once:** (1) the AI capex fear is now in the price, (2) midterm uncertainty clears in weeks, (3) M2 money supply is growing at its fastest pace in ~4 years and the Fed has stopped shrinking its balance sheet and is buying short-term debt again. "Cheap, plus fear lifting, plus a wall of money."
- **Midterm seasonality:** the S&P 500 averaged ~16% in the 12 months after a midterm and ~14% in the first 6 months.
- **What the skilled money does:** follows money flows, not stories. It buys quality (growing sales, growing profits, good return on capital) while the crowd is scared, and it does not sit in idle cash that inflation eats.
- **Source caveat:** the video is a funnel for a free live webinar (midtermsinvesting.com) and the Winston stock app (30-day trial, "Founders Tier"). YouTube files it under *Entertainment*. Read it as a well-argued market commentary from someone with something to sell, not as research. See [[#🔍 Critical read — claim check and caveats]].

## 🧩 Act 1 — The bear market under a green index

[2:29](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=149s)

| Figure | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **6 in 10** | US stocks down more than 20% from their highs, the technical definition of a bear market |
| **9 in 10** | chip makers down more than 20%, the "can't-lose" AI trade |
| **4 in 10** | chip makers that have lost half their value |
| **~40%** | weight of a handful of giant tech names in the S&P 500 today |
| **~26%** | weight of the top 10 at the dot-com peak in 2000 |

- **The index is not 500 companies any more. It is about 8.** When those few go up, they drag the headline number with them while most other names bleed out of sight.
- **Passive investors are less diversified than they think.** If you bought the index and tucked it away, you are leaning on a handful of names that all do roughly the same thing. When one of them trips (a bad earnings call, a management blunder), the whole basket wobbles. Prehn expects that wobble to come for the big 8 at some point. They "have yet to be kicked properly".
- **Why it is hope, not fear:** most of the damage, for most of the market, has already happened. The open question is what comes next and who gets paid.
- **The money will not come back into everything.** It pours into one kind of company and leaves the rest. The difference is being positioned before it moves, not after.

## 🧩 Act 2 — Why the midterms are the trigger

[7:31](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=451s)

- **Elections open the spending taps.** More promises, programmes and borrowing. Part of that money lands in the real economy and part ends up in the market, bidding up asset prices.
- **The pattern:** the S&P 500 has averaged **~16%** in the 12 months after a midterm and **~14%** in the first 6 months. It has finished higher in the year after a midterm "again and again", tied to the most predictable event on the calendar.
- **Why it works: an election clears the air.** Markets hate not knowing more than they hate bad news. An open question freezes money on the sidelines. Once the vote is done, that cash looks for a home all at once.
- **The money moves first, the real economy follows.** Wages, egg prices and the job market lag a long way behind. Get the direction of the money right and you are ahead of people who are cleverer than you but busy worrying about the wrong thing.
- **The 2023 lesson:** half the experts promised a recession that never came. The headlines stayed grim and the market had one of its best years in a decade, because the money turned before the news did.

## 🧩 Act 3 — Three forces pushing the same way

[10:05](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=605s)

1. **The AI fear clears.** For two years big tech poured hundreds of billions into AI, and the market worried whether it would pay off and how it was financed. That worry is why most stocks fell. A wave of spending like that does not vanish. It flows to the picks-and-shovels suppliers (chips, power, data centres) and then to the companies that use the technology to make real money. *The fear comes first and the payoff comes after.*
   - **We have seen this film:** railways, electricity, the internet. Everyone piles in, they overbuild, the crowd panics, the weak players get washed out, and the survivors make a fortune. "The internet did not die in 2000. The nonsense did." Prehn puts AI somewhere in the washout now.
2. **The question mark lifts.** Whichever way the midterm goes, the uncertainty is gone within weeks.
3. **The taps turn back on**, the one he would "circle twice":
   - **M2** (roughly, the amount of money in the US system) is growing at its fastest pace in ~4 years.
   - **The Fed has stopped shrinking its balance sheet** and started buying government debt again through the short-term debt market. Prehn calls it "a vitamin shot into the market's arm": it does not cure anything, but it perks things up for a while.
   - That money looks for a return and usually ends up in stocks and in hard assets that hold value while cash loses it.

> **Put the three together:** most stocks have already fallen, so they are cheap. The fear that pushed them down is clearing. Fresh money is arriving at the fastest rate in years. That is the setup Mike Wilson is reading when he calls it one of the best of his career.

## 🧩 Act 4 — What the "skilled money" does

[13:41](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=821s)

- **"Skilled", not "smart".** Prehn: "I've met the bankers, I was one of them. We're not that smart, but we're skilled." They have been trained, and they have rules they follow whether they feel like it or not.
- **They follow the money.** They watch where it flows and go there. Nobody inside a fund buys and holds a stock "till the end of time". The market is "a live democracy where the money gets to vote", the ultimate popularity contest.
- **The flood goes into quality, not the bin.** Companies growing sales and profits and earning a decent return on capital, or the occasional story so exciting that everyone wants it anyway. Many fallen names fell for a reason. Skilled money is not bargain-hunting in the rubbish. It buys good businesses while the crowd is too scared to look.
- **The crowd is scared, and that is bullish.** The AAII sentiment survey has asked since the 1980s whether the market will be higher or lower in 6 months:

| Reading | Bearish | What followed |
|---------|--------:|---------------|
| Long-run average | ~31% | — |
| Early 2009 | 70%+ | market rose ~67% over the next year |
| Autumn 2022 | ~60% | market climbed from there |
| Lately | well above average | — |

- **Why it works:** a frightened person has usually already sold. When nearly everyone has sold, there is hardly anyone left to sell, and a mountain of cash is waiting to come back. "The best time to buy almost always feels horrible." See the AAII row in [[Bear Markets — 100 Years of History#🧩 Part IV — Indicators — a Compass, Not a Clock|the indicator compass]].

## 🧩 The market is not the economy

[16:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=990s)

The mistake to avoid: reading shaky economic news and assuming the market must crash, as if the stock market were a thermometer for the economy. It is not. It prices what companies will earn tomorrow and how much money is sloshing around looking for a home.

| Year | The economy | The market | Why |
|------|-------------|------------|-----|
| 2020 | unemployment near 15%, worst since the Great Depression | at a record high by August | the system was flooded with money and it went straight into stocks |
| 2022 | jobs fine, unemployment near a 50-year low | fell 19% | the taps were turned off and money was pulled out of the system |

It runs both ways: **bad economic news can be good news for the market**, because it is what gets the money printers going again.

## 🧩 The plan and the 5-question checklist

[18:03](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=1083s)

Two things, "and neither of them is panic" (explicitly framed as not financial advice):

1. **Safe is not safe.** Do not sit in a big pile of cash feeling safe. The same money printing that lifts the market eats your cash. Prehn measures money in hours of your life, and idle cash is "a slow, quiet loss you never get a statement for".
2. **Look at quality, not the news.** Stop reading the economy as a market signal. Three boring questions per business: **Are sales growing? Are profits growing? Is it earning a good return on what it owns?** Get those right across a handful of names and, he claims, you beat most professional fund managers. Also: stop reading newspapers, cancel Netflix, turn off the TV, and learn the skill instead of listening to "some lunatic on social media".

**The checklist** (PDF, p. 11), to hold up against your own money:

- [ ] If I own the index, do I realise a handful of giant names make up ~40% of it, and most of the rest have already fallen?
- [ ] How much am I holding in idle cash that money printing is slowly eating?
- [ ] For the businesses I own: are sales growing, are profits growing, and do they earn a good return on what they use?
- [ ] Am I buying quality while the crowd is frightened, or waiting for an all-clear in the headlines that always arrives too late?
- [ ] Am I arranging my money before the midterms and the months straight after, rather than reacting once it is in the papers?

> "You do not need to score full marks today. You need to know where you stand, and move in the right direction while it is still early."

## 🔍 Critical read — claim check and caveats

The video and PDF were published in late October 2026. I could not verify the current-market figures (breadth, M2 growth, AAII "lately") against live data here, so treat them as the source's claims. The PDF cites investinglive.com, morganstanley.com, economictimes, commonfund.org, BlackRock, Fool.com, Fidelity, AAII and wiserinvestor.com.

| Claim | Status |
|-------|--------|
| AAII bears 70%+ in early 2009 | ✅ Matches the 70.3% (5 Mar 2009) reading in [[Bear Markets — 100 Years of History]] |
| AAII bears ~60% in autumn 2022 | ✅ PDF says 60%, which matches the late-September 2022 reading. ⚠️ The video says **67%** at [16:03](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GSDnRo7IuuU&t=963s), a slip (67% is the 2009-rally figure). |
| Market up after "every single midterm going back 50 years" | ⚠️ The video says *every single*. The PDF softens it to *nearly every*. It is a widely cited seasonal pattern, but the sample is small (about 20 midterm cycles since WWII) and averages hide the spread. A pattern, not a law. |
| 2020: unemployment ~15%, S&P record high by August | ✅ Consistent with April 2020 (~14.7%) and the August 2020 record close |
| 2022: unemployment near a 50-year low, S&P −19% | ✅ Consistent with the S&P 500's ~−19% calendar 2022 |
| Fed stopped QT and is buying short-term debt again | ✅ Consistent with QT ending on 1 Dec 2025 and T-bill reserve-management purchases starting that month. Prehn frames it as stimulus. The Fed frames it as plumbing (keeping reserves ample), not QE. |
| Top 10 ~40% of the S&P 500 vs ~26% in 2000 | ✅ In line with widely cited concentration data. Note the switch between "top 10" (the stat) and "about 8" (the slogan). |
| "6 in 10 US stocks in a bear market" | ❓ Unverified here. It depends on the universe (all US listings vs S&P 500). Small caps inflate this kind of breadth stat. |

**Source caveats:**

- **It is a sales funnel.** Three calls to action: the free live webinar (midtermsinvesting.com, "no replay"), the Winston app trial with a "Founders Tier", and the free PDF behind an email gate. The PDF says "there is nothing to buy" while promoting the app trial. Urgency framing ("the window is the next few weeks", "most people will find out after it's over") is a classic scarcity hook. Prehn himself lists "how to tell real scarcity from the fake kind" as a webinar topic.
- **"Winston, Lead Analyst"** on the PDF byline is the name of Prehn's dog and of his app. It is branding, not a named research analyst.
- **Prehn contradicts himself on time horizon.** He says "the skilled money follows the money" and does not buy and hold, then praises quality investing in a handful of businesses. He knocks the index ("how's that working out for you?"), then concedes it has worked for 30–40 years.
- **Survivorship in the "we have seen this film" analogy.** Railways and dot-coms did produce winners, but many investors in those booms lost for a decade or more. Knowing that a washout ends is not the same as knowing when it ends or who survives it.
- **"Bad news is good news" cuts both ways.** It is true while liquidity is expanding. It flips the moment the taps close, which is his own 2022 example.

## ☘️ What I take from it

- **The concentration point hits me twice.** My portfolio is tech stocks + tech ETFs + BTC/ETH. That is the same long-duration, liquidity-sensitive bet, as [[My Investment Strategy]] already admits. Prehn's "you own 8 stocks, not 500" applies even more to tech ETFs than to the S&P 500. Worth a look at what my ETFs actually hold.
- **His "don't sit in cash" is not my cash cushion.** My cushion is ballast and dry powder for a capitulation, not a market call. The inflation-drag argument applies to *excess* idle cash beyond the cushion, and that is a reason to finally fill the IKE/IKZE room, not to drain the buffer.
- **Midterm seasonality is context, not a trigger.** It fits the "compass, not a clock" rule from [[Bear Markets — 100 Years of History]]. I will not change my allocation because of a calendar pattern with ~20 data points.
- **The three quality questions are a good filter** for any individual stock I consider: sales growth, profit growth, return on capital. Simple enough to actually run every time.
- **Sentiment as contrarian context** lines up with what I already wrote in [[Investing Psychology]]: extreme AAII bear readings are worth noticing, never a date.
- **Liquidity (M2, Fed balance sheet) is the driver I track least.** My notes cover valuation, credit spreads and sentiment, but not money supply. Prehn's 2020 vs 2022 table is a good reminder that liquidity often beats fundamentals in the short run. #todo Add M2 / Fed balance sheet to the indicator compass I glance at quarterly.
