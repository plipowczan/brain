# Mechanical Crash Thursday — Cymcyk on Buying Panic

## 🗒️ Description

An 18-minute Polish YouTube video by Piotr Cymcyk (channel "Merytorycznie o Finansach", brand DNA Rynków), published the day after a violent US selloff on Thursday 5 November 2026. By his numbers: Nasdaq about −6% in one day, semiconductors about −12%, and the VIX up about 70%. Media were asking who predicted the crash and what disasters come next. His answer is that he does not care, because he has watched this for 17 years.

His argument has three legs. First, such days are statistically followed by gains. Second, this one looked mechanical (forced selling and rule-based funds) rather than fundamental. Third, AI and chip demand is not the bubble the headlines suggest, and chip valuations have already been cut hard. The closing message is about psychology: you earn on being brave, not on predicting crashes.

I read it as a useful companion to [[Midterms 2026 Market Setup (Felix Prehn)]], which made a similar "the fear is priced in" case a week earlier. Both are bullish voices selling something, so I keep the claim check below.

(Auto-captions garble the tickers: "NZD" is Nasdaq, "Wix" is VIX. About 3.5 minutes at the start are sponsor reads.)

🚀 **TL;DR**

- **Extreme but not unprecedented.** A one-day VIX jump of +70% has happened only twice before in 36 years of the index (Feb 2018, Dec 2024). Jumps above 40% happened about 25 times since 1990, roughly once per 1.5 years.
- **Looked like a flush, not a verdict.** Open high, sharp slide, VIX nearly doubling intraday: the pattern of a big player closing positions regardless of price. Bonds, dollar and gold stayed calm; only equities, mostly chips and AI names, fell.
- **History is on the buyer's side, with caveats.** Since 1985 the S&P 500 had 53 days of −4% or worse. A year later it was higher 83% of the time, average +24%.
- **Chips are cheap on paper.** SOX forward P/E fell from about 28× to about 16× in two months, and to 9.9× on 2-year-forward earnings, the first single digit since 2011.
- **Demand looks locked in through 2027.** The open question is 2028, and a delay would flatten the cycle rather than kill it.
- **The real lesson:** nobody earns by predicting crashes. More money is lost waiting for them than during them.

### The mechanics: why it fell

His reading of the session, in four pieces:

1. **Shape of the day.** A relatively high open, then a sharp drop, with the VIX nearly doubling. In his experience that is a big holder forced to close fast, with price irrelevant.
2. **The leverage spiral.** Stocks fall, the broker issues a margin call, the fund has no cash, sells, the selling deepens the fall. The seller is not asking whether Nvidia is cheap. They need cash now, and the buyer on the other side gets the price as a gift, if they hold spare cash and nerve.
3. **Hedge-fund liquidations.** He cites Mura (the Polish broker's note after the session) on a race to lock in 2026 gains, and Wanda Research on forced selling by leveraged and systematic strategies. Named funds in circulation: Engineers Gate, Lone Pine, Millennium and Verition pods, and one more (the caption is garbled). Combined assets of "several dozen billion dollars" before leverage. These names come from online chatter after the fact, so treat them as rumour.
4. **CTA funds.** Trend-following Commodity Trading Advisors set positions from price alone, with rules in code. Their US-equity positioning was −3.3 standard deviations on 3 November (lower in under 2% of the last year), and they had been building shorts for about 26 sessions. His point: most of their selling is probably done, and the market only needs to flatten, not rally, for them to flip from ultra-short to ultra-long. He compares it to late March 2026, when the same gauge touched −4: the S&P 500 bottomed on 30 March and rose 23% by October (Nasdaq +33%).

### What history says

His three data cuts, all on the S&P 500 unless noted:

| Sample | Result a year later |
|---|---|
| 53 sessions of −4% or worse since 1985 | higher in 83% of cases, average +24% |
| Same, but only when the index was under 10% from its peak | positive after 3 and 6 months in 100% of cases, median +20% at 6 months |
| Nasdaq: 21 sessions of −5% or worse since 2008 | higher after a year every time, median +43% |

He pre-empts the obvious objection himself: 21 of the 53 sessions fell in the 2008 bear market, which is why he ran the near-peak cut. The S&P was under 6% from its record before Thursday. He also notes the S&P is far less volatile than the Nasdaq, so the Nasdaq numbers come from a different regime.

### Is it an AI bubble

- **Valuation reset.** The SOX forward P/E went from 28× two months ago to a little over 16×, a 40% contraction. On earnings two years out it is 9.9×, the first single digit since 2011. Chips trade at roughly a 35% discount to the S&P 500, the largest in at least 15 years. His reading: investors did not just sell, they changed what they will pay for future chip earnings. "They did not believe the forecasts before either, then apologised. This song has played several times."
- **Demand is mostly booked.** The real question mark is 2028, not 2027, since orders, budgets and prepayments for 2027 are done. He cites SemiAnalysis: about 75% of the capacity planned for next year in the states analysed is already under construction, with permits, grid connections and poured concrete.
- **Politics.** He dismisses the fear that Democrats will "kill data centres": Virginia, the top data-centre state, has long been Democratic-run, and in Texas the decision sits with a regulator staffed by Republican appointees. Governors can delay, not stop. A half-year slip in 2028 projects would shift analyst forecasts from 2028 to 2029: a lower peak but a longer cycle, which he calls arguably better because it lowers the classic overbuild risk that historically ended chip cycles.
- **Anthropic.** His premium members got a detailed walk-through of Anthropic's S-1 prospectus and Q3 numbers. The last update disappointed, with growth slowing more than expected. He says the newest data answers that: run-rate revenue above \$80B at end of October (Ticker Trend estimates \$82B), +7% month on month, an acceleration, leaving a path to \$90B+ by December. He cannot imagine a productivity revolution that does not need more chips, power and compute.

### Psychology and the close

- "Fear has big eyes, but you earn on fear only briefly. You earn longer on greed, meaning buying." Capital does not tolerate a vacuum.
- Big moves mean nothing most of the time. The first thing to do is notice how extreme such days are: under five years of investing means you may never have seen one (the last was in 2020).
- "On the exchange you do not earn on predicting crashes. More money is lost waiting for crashes than during them. You earn on being brave."
- The test of Thursday was the investor, not the valuations: did you click buy or panic?
- In a month nobody will remember it, the VIX will be back under 20, and the media will be heating up something else.

## ☘️ What I take from it

- The **mechanism** section is the most valuable part: forced liquidation and rule-based CTA flows explain big index moves without any information about fundamentals. It fits the way [[Bear Markets — 100 Years of History]] separates bear types and the way [[Investing Psychology]] frames panic as the real cost.
- The **statistics** are fair as a base rate and badly framed as a forecast (see the claim check). I would use them to calm myself, not to size a position.
- It reinforces what I already hold in [[My Investment Strategy]]: have cash set aside before the drop, decide the rules in advance, and do not trade on the day. The video adds the point that "cash plus nerve" is the actual scarce resource, which maps onto [[Cognitive Biases in Investing]] (loss aversion, herding) and the rebalancing logic in [[Asset Allocation and Diversification]].
- Compare with the Prehn video: [[Midterms 2026 Market Setup (Felix Prehn)]] argues a slow-build setup (breadth, liquidity, election) while this one argues a sudden mechanical flush. Both land on "do not sell the bottom".

## ✍️ Claim check

I could not independently verify any of the market numbers. They come from the video, and the dates are recent (the event was the day before publication), so I flag everything as unverified.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Nasdaq −6%, SOX −12%, VIX +70% on 5 Nov 2026 | Speaker's figures, unverified |
| VIX +70% beaten only in Feb 2018 and Dec 2024 | Plausible and consistent with the two known spikes (Feb 2018, Dec 2024), not checked against data |
| 53 days of −4%+ since 1985, 83% higher after a year, avg +24% | Unverified. **Clustered**: many days come from 1987, 2008, 2020 and 2022, so the 53 are not independent observations |
| Near-peak subset: 100% positive at 3 and 6 months, median +20% | Sample size not given, and probably small, so "100%" is weak evidence |
| Nasdaq: higher after a year after each of 21 days, median +43% | Unverified; 21 events in a few clusters |
| Named hedge funds were liquidated | Online rumour, not confirmed; one name garbled in captions |
| CTA gauge −3.3σ, shorts built over 26 sessions | Source of the model not named |
| SOX forward P/E 28× → 16×, 9.9× at 2-year-forward, 35% discount | Unverified; forward P/E on collapsing estimates can look cheap for the wrong reason |
| 75% of next-year capacity under construction (SemiAnalysis) | Attributed, not checked |
| Anthropic run-rate above \$80B, +7% MoM | Unverified; the \$80B+ figure comes from Ticker Trend-type estimates, not an audited filing in my sources |

**Caveats on the source.**

- **Sales funnel.** The first 3.5 minutes are sponsor reads: eToro (a \$50 bonus via his link), Lantre (a discount code), a coffee-mug promotion, and the paid DNA premium zone (6,300+ members, reports a portfolio of just under PLN 10M). The ending is a pitch to join it. He benefits when viewers stay calm and invested, and when they buy the platform he is paid by.
- **Survivorship and framing.** "It was always an opportunity" is a base rate from an index that kept recovering. It says nothing about a single stock like Nvidia, a leveraged position, or a crash that does not recover for years.
- **Two conclusions he did not earn.** (1) That the flush was mechanical is inferred from the day's shape and from after-the-fact chatter. (2) That "the AI bubble did not burst" does not follow from an Anthropic revenue update.
- Not financial advice, including mine.
