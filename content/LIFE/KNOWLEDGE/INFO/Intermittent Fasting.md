# Intermittent Fasting

> [!info] Personal context
> I do a **weekly 24-hour fast** and wanted to know: is it worth continuing? This is the fact-checked answer — RCTs, network meta-analyses (mostly 2024–2025), each claim adversarially verified. I also have [[Hashimoto and Diet|Hashimoto's]], which adds a thyroid caveat. **Not medical advice.**

**Evidence key:** 🟢 strong (RCTs/meta-analyses) · 🟡 moderate · 🔴 weak/unproven.

## 🗒️ The blunt answer

**IF is not metabolically magic. 🟢** Its benefits come almost entirely from **helping you eat less**, not from meal timing or fasting per se. When calories are matched (isocaloric trials), **IF is no better than ordinary daily calorie restriction** for weight, fat loss, or disease-risk markers. And nearly all the evidence comes from **overweight/obese** people — as a lean, healthy adult I should expect **even less** upside. The autophagy/longevity story that sells fasting is **unproven in humans**.

So: my weekly 24h fast is **fine as a discipline/eating-window tool if I like it**, but it isn't delivering hidden cellular benefits, and it carries a thyroid caveat worth watching.

## 🧩 Weight & fat loss 🟢

- Largest evidence base — BMJ 2025 **network meta-analysis, 99 RCTs / 6,582 adults**: only **alternate-day fasting** beat daily calorie restriction, and by a **trivial −1.29 kg** (below the ~2 kg clinically-meaningful threshold). At **≥24 weeks, no IF strategy beat plain calorie restriction at all**.[^bmj]
- Vs unrestricted eating, every approach gave **similar modest loss** (ADF −3.4, whole-day-fast −2.4, daily restriction −2.1, 16:8 TRE −1.7 kg) — i.e. the **deficit drives it, not the timing**.[^bmj]
- Isocaloric trials confirm: **IF = calorie restriction**, benefits are "calorie-restriction-dependent."[^iso][^nmcd]
- **Counterpoint 🟡:** one 12-month RCT (4:3 fasting, n=165) had IF beat matched daily restriction by **~2.9 kg** (−7.7 vs −4.8 kg) — but it's a single trial with **uneven dropout** (19% vs 30%), not a body of evidence.[^annals]
- Small isocaloric edge for IF on **fat mass, waist, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR** 🟡 — isolated, low-confidence signals; calorie restriction beat IF on hunger and triglycerides.[^nmcd]

## 🧩 Metabolic health (non-diabetic adults) 🟢

Honest summary: **small, real, but modest — and concentrated in people with abnormal baselines (not lean me).**
- **Blood pressure:** TRE lowers it ~−1.8/−1.75 mmHg — but the effect is **driven by hypertensives; null in normotensive people**.[^bp]
- **Glucose/insulin:** small drops in fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR — **low-to-moderate certainty**; HbA1c unchanged (except trials >6 months). Likely partly an inadvertent calorie deficit.[^bp][^nutrev]
- **Lipids:** **barely move** without actual calorie reduction.[^bp][^nutrev]
- **Inflammation:** partial — lowers TNF-α and CRP (not IL-6), but **CRP only drops with >6% weight loss**, and reviewers can't separate fasting from the weight loss.[^inflam]

## 🧩 Autophagy — mostly hype in humans 🔴

The headline reason people fast. The verified picture:
- **No confirmed human evidence** that fasting of any duration triggers health-relevant autophagy. The one human time-restricted-eating trial showed a **borderline, post-hoc** signal (P=0.04 at 6 months) with **no rise above the group's own baseline**.[^autophagy]
- A dedicated **24-hour-fast autophagy trial was withdrawn with zero participants enrolled** — illustrating how thin the direct human data is.[^autonct]
- The famous autophagy/Ohsumi Nobel work is **yeast and mice**. Extrapolating "16h/24h fasting cleans your cells" to humans is **not supported**.

## 🧩 Longevity & the cardiovascular scare 🔴

- **Lifespan extension from IF is shown in animals, not humans.** No verified human longevity or mortality evidence surfaced.
- The 2024 **"8h TRE → 91% higher cardiovascular death"** headline was an **AHA conference abstract** (not peer-reviewed), **observational** (NHANES, based on **two days** of self-reported diet), and was **heavily criticized**.[^cvmort] Low credibility — neither proves harm nor got confirmed here. Don't over-weight it either way.

## 🧩 24-hour fasting specifically + my thyroid 🟡/🔴

- The evidence consistently attributes effects to **energy reduction, not timing**, and **does not isolate** once-weekly 24h fasts or OMAD as special vs 16:8. No reason to believe a 24h fast beats a simpler eating window for a healthy person.
- **Thyroid caveat (from my [[Hashimoto and Diet]] research):** a single 24h fast shifts thyroid hormones in healthy people (**fT3 ↓~6%, reverse-T3 ↑~16%**) — a mild "low-T3" pattern that reverses on refeeding. Ramadan-style fasting kept ~80% of treated-hypothyroid patients euthyroid. With **Hashimoto's**, the long-term effect of repeated 24h fasts is **unstudied**.
- **Risks not covered by this research (🔴, flag honestly):** muscle/lean-mass loss, women's hormonal/menstrual disruption, disordered-eating risk, gallstones — none verified here. Generally: IF is **not advised** for anyone underweight, pregnant/breastfeeding, with a history of eating disorders, or (caution) with thyroid disease.

## 📒 My verdict

- **Keep it only if I genuinely like it** as an eating-discipline tool. It will not give me autophagy/longevity magic — that's unproven in humans.
- **Don't expect metabolic gains** as a lean person; the benefits live in dysmetabolic populations.
- **Watch the thyroid:** track TSH/fT4 across cycles (already my plan from [[Hashimoto and Diet]]); ensure adequate protein on eating days to protect muscle.
- If the real goal is body composition or energy, **meal quality + protein + a moderate deficit** (see [[Glucose Spikes and Performance]]) does the same job without the timing dogma.
