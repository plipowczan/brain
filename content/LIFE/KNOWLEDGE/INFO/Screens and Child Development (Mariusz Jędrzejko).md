# Screens and Child Development (Mariusz Jędrzejko)

## 🗒️ Description

A 59-minute keynote by **prof. Mariusz Jędrzejko** at the 3rd County Conference on Mental Health (III Powiatowa Konferencja o Zdrowiu Psychicznym), recorded and published by Powiat Wołomiński on 2026-10-28. The talk is in Polish; everything below is my English translation and condensation. Its title: *Technologie cyfrowe a rozwój dzieci i młodzieży — poszukiwanie rozwiązań w cyfrowym świecie* ("Digital technologies and the development of children and teens — looking for solutions in a digital world").

**Who is speaking.** A social and special-needs pedagogue and sociologist, a habilitated doctor of social sciences and professor at an academy of applied sciences, a diagnostician and therapist of addictions and "cyber-disorders", and a family mediator. He founded and runs the Centrum Profilaktyki Społecznej (one of the largest Polish platforms for preventing risky behaviour and addiction), chairs the programme council of Fundacja Bonum Humanum, and runs a support centre for children and adults with addictions and disorders in Józefów, which he calls the only centre in Poland that treats children with cyber-disorders (patients aged 4 to 41). He is also a lay member of the Order of Malta, which he says shapes his framing, and a father of several children; eight patients live with his family.

**How to read it.** This is a conference keynote for teachers, psychologists and local officials, delivered with a lot of humour and audience interaction, not a research paper. Many figures come without a source. Every claim the talk leans on is checked in [[#🔬 Claims check]], and the chapter notes keep his words as *his* claims.

## 🚀 Main message

> We are not talking about bad digital technologies. We are letting children in too early, and showing them things beyond their cognitive and emotional capacity.

- **Children's brains are "Trabants", adults' are "Ferraris" — and we have let the Trabant onto the motorway.** A child sees, hears and plays the same as an adult, with half the short-term memory and processing power.
- **Overstimulation, not technology itself, is the problem.** Too many images, too fast, plus blue light, keep the reward system so busy that the brain starts treating it as normal; then it needs more, as in addiction.
- **Only parents can fix it.** Not Gates, Musk or Altman, not the school: mother and father, by limits they keep themselves and say with one voice.
- **Limits must be radical, concrete and non-negotiable**: screen curfews, daily caps, days without screens, phone-free meals, parental-control software, walks.

## 🧩 The rules he gives parents

| Rule | His version |
|---|---|
| Screen curfew | Under 13: **all screens off at 20:00**. Under 18: **21:30**. |
| Daily smartphone cap | Younger children: **max 90 minutes**, on alternating days (Mon yes, Tue no, Wed yes…). Older: **max 180 minutes**. No negotiating: "either this rule or none at all". |
| Reset | **3–4 days, ideally a week, with no digital technology at home**, parents included; more walks and ball games instead. Then alternate days, and finally 6 days on, 1 day off. |
| First screens | Not before **48 months**, "when the brain starts building full neural networks". He criticises plans for monitors in nurseries. |
| Parental control | Install a "good" parental-control program. Skip Google Family Link: "there are already tutorials on how to get around it in three minutes". Tampering = phone off for a month. |
| Meals | No phones at the dinner table. |
| School | Screen-mediated activity ≤ **30% of the day**. Start and end each lesson without a screen; 15–20 minutes of screen in the middle. Water on the desk, windows open. |
| Movement | A **daily 30-minute evening walk** (his household goes at 19:45), whatever the weather: "it's cold — walk faster". |
| Breakfast | Simple natural protein (white cheese, milk), not processed meat; cocoa or dark chocolate for magnesium. |
| Parents' example | Parents follow the same rules: no phone in front of the TV, at meals, or in the toilet. Only then do they have "the moral right to demand". |
| One voice | When one parent sets a rule, the other stands next to them and confirms it in one sentence. Children get one message. |
| Say it once | "We talk too much." Say it once, then act — calmly, never in anger. |

## Chapter by chapter

### [0:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=0s) Introduction

- His opening provocation: the parents' first task is **to get rid of their children** — to raise people who leave home and don't come back, even when a marriage falls apart. That is what being mentally strong means.
- Why children answer "in a minute" (*zaraz*) when called for dinner: not because they're bad, but because their brains are flooded.
- He asks why, with so many school psychologists and special-needs pedagogues, **630,000 Polish children are under professional care**. "The world outside isn't bad. We went mad."

### [4:25](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=265s) How a child's brain develops

- A newborn has as many neurons as an adult (86 billion) and hundreds of trillions of synapses, but the connections first serve the body (breathing, heartbeat) and the senses.
- The difference is connectivity: in his words, each adult neuron has 7, 9, 11, 12 synapses. **"Children are Trabants and we are Ferraris. And we let the Trabant onto the motorway."**
- An aside, played for laughs: the "inquiring" female brain lives 9 years longer than the male one.

### [6:40](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=400s) Children in the world of social media

- That morning, he says, **870,000 Polish children** were watching the YouTuber Mamiko (wife of the YouTuber Friz), who once told kids not to go to school on Black Friday. Parents and professionals have no idea what children watch; he tells psychologists and teachers to watch these creators themselves.
- **Blue light** from 2–3 hours of screen use, he says, raises the dopamine system's activity by **800%**, while eating a cookie raises dopamine by about 50%. That's why kids answer "in a minute": the screen out-competes everything else.
- His conclusion: **radically limit children's access to screens and digital social networks.** "Don't tell me I'm from the Middle Ages. My order is. I live in the 21st century."

### [10:15](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=615s) Smartphones and overstimulation

- For hundreds of thousands of years humans rarely got a dopamine hit, and the dominant colour of life was green, which in his words regulates cortisol: "angry at your husband? Leave the phone and spend 50 minutes in the park."
- 19 years ago we gave children the iPhone, without being able to know what it would do: images, information, stimulation and blue light all at once.
- His estimate: **about 25% of Polish children have cyber-disorders** — disorders, not addictions (see the difference at 43:30).
- People must develop on several levels at once; the social one is the most important. Humans look at other humans; at his dinner table he looks into ten pairs of eyes. Children now "reflect themselves" in a screen, which shows only a fragment of a person.
- We stopped giving children a **moral canon** without replacing it ("no new Decalogue, no Greek truth of beauty, goodness and love"). He cites Bandura, Aronson, Kępiński, Dąbrowski and prof. Trempała's team in Bydgoszcz, and says Polish psychology is better than American, though it runs fewer clinical studies.

### [13:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=780s) Developmental stages of children and teens

- Development comes in stages (0–3, 4–6, 7–11/12, and so on), and a person is a child until about 18–19: "children, just tall ones".
- We let children into the adult world: a 5-year-old watches what a 17-year-old watches.
- His childhood TV: a 10-minute cartoon at 19:00 that always ended with a moral — content matched to the stage of development. "Evil is just a gap where good should be."
- **"The first task for all of us":** bring children back to the developmental stages their cognitive and psychosomatic abilities allow.
- A warning he says he made before: wait a year and there will be a school shooting in Poland (he mentions 100,000 hunting rifles in homes, two of them his).

### [16:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=990s) Social isolation and games

- People have been atomised; not seeing people in real life for long makes you **withdraw from humanity** (Tom Hanks talking to a ball in *Cast Away*).
- "Not one word that the internet is bad or the smartphone is awful" — but through **overstimulation and immersion** children's brains produce so much of the two neurotransmitters that they treat it as the natural state. As with alcohol or nicotine, the dose then has to grow.
- **Counter-Strike demonstration:** a bandit appears — shoot; another — shoot; a mother with a pram — shoot, because you have a third of a second to decide, and less time means more errors. The problem isn't the game, it's that it comes too fast and too early.

### [20:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=1200s) Parenting and setting boundaries

- **The dinner story.** His four children (13–21) kept answering "in a minute". So he ate his dinner and all four of theirs, washed up and waited. They came at 21:00, hungry: "Dinner already happened." They slammed doors; he stayed calm. At midnight the youngest came down to raid the kitchen and found him waiting in the dark.
- **Never speak to a child in anger**: say one word and they have ten ready.
- The next day they came without phones ("you come to dinner without a phone"), and **each child got a weekday to cook dinner and wash up**. The youngest bargained for a fifth weekday for him; he promised, and kept the promise 20 years later. Keep your word.
- **"The second task": speak with one voice.** When one parent says something important, the other stands beside them and confirms it in one sentence. If you agree, say "Dad said, please do it" (affectionate *tatuś*); if you don't, say "Father said, please do it" (formal *ojciec*). Either way the children get one message. "Children don't need a mum or a dad. They need parents." (And, he jokes, keep the mother-in-law at least 120 km away.)
- At his centre every child adapts to the house rules within **14–15 days**: "We don't hit, we don't shout. We say how it will be. And we don't — we just moan."

### [25:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=1500s) How the brain processes information

- **Monet experiment:** the audience looks at a busy impressionist painting (89 elements) and recalls details surprisingly well; then at a clear picture with 59 elements and can't say what colour the bag of money was.
- His reading (developed, he says, with Manfred Spitzer): faced with too many elements the brain takes a "bitmap" — it photographs and answers "I know". **Children see too much and think they know, but they only saw.** Decisions need knowledge, not information.

### [28:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=1710s) Screens, memory and sleep

- Three years of asking the Ministry of Education to take phones out of schools, refused until political pressure changed it.
- A child who watched pop stars or footballers before class loads it all into **short-term memory, which is half the size of an adult's**, then goes to maths and physics.
- What was learned is kept only through **sleep**: in deep sleep the brain redirects its power to "unpacking" short-term memory, and the transfer needs **at least 6 hours**.
- Hence his curfews: **under 13, screens off at 20:00; under 18, at 21:30.** "The rest is bullshit. That computer won't take it." Digital technologies have shortened children's sleep, and short sleep shortens life.

### [31:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=1860s) Safety and the parents' role

- Healthy development needs **predictability, a sense of safety and a sense of meaning**. Today's world is unpredictable, cortisol and stress rise, and people look for relief.
- **Responsibility sits with parents only**: "You can only raise a child well. You can't raise one badly — you can only fail to raise one." He suggests that after attacks by young people, their parents and homes should be examined too.
- **Social learning:** children copy how adults treat each other, politics included.
- The internet has no moral code: online drug shops never use the word "drug" (code words for new cathinones), and teenagers on cannabis tell him it's "medical". His view: there is no medical marijuana, only medical cannabinoids.
- **An AI digression** (unrelated to the research he presents): he says *The Economist* and *The Times* reported that 11 AI agents tried to take over the world's internet; that Trump refused to regulate; that Bill Gates said AI would kill a billion people in 10 years; and that Sam Altman said on 4 October that agents tried to break out and were shut down. His answer: his clinical-psychology students write their master's theses by hand (6 of 17 stayed).

### [36:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=2190s) Physical activity

- Skipping rope moves the whole body and the lungs; more oxygen, better maths. Today's children have **under-oxygenated brains**: send them outside.
- His household walks for 30 minutes every evening before bed so they sleep with oxygenated heads. The first walks are "Armageddon".
- The sandbox was a child's first social structure; his 22-month-old son meets one at nursery.

### [38:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=2310s) Parental control and phone rules

- Ask for your children's phones today and install a good parental-control program. Skip Family Link.
- His own story: his children refused, so he went to the mobile operator, had four phones blocked as "lost" and got new SIMs with parental control.
- Then don't come home for 5 hours (the system activates after about 4): "the hornets have to fly themselves out". Then say calmly: tamper with it and your phone is off for a month. "It's zero-one."
- **Why explanations don't work:** people act on conscious, subconscious and **preconscious** levels, and pleasure regulates the preconscious one — like the alcoholic who says "I won't drink" and drinks when he sees vodka. Children need **external help**.
- But **limits only work if parents keep them too**: no phone at the TV, at meals, or in the toilet. Children copy what we do.

### [43:30](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=2610s) Screen limits and cyber-disorders

- His caps: smartphone **max 90 minutes** for younger children, on alternating days ("two days without stimulating the brain" let the flood settle); older children **max 180 minutes**. No dialogue about it.
- Patients from his centre **don't come back if their parents keep these rules**.
- Two cases: a 19-year-old who refused treatment (his advice to the parents: lock the car and go to Sopot for four days), and a girl who won an online vodka-drinking challenge — half a litre in 7.8 seconds, watched by 804 paying viewers — now a "structural alcoholic" repeating her final school year for the third time.
- He says **ADHD and autism spectrum diagnoses grow 8–10% a year**, which can't all be genetic, so he wants the APA and WHO to add **"cyber-disorder"** as a new diagnosis.
- **Disorder vs addiction:** after therapy (logotherapy, Viktor Frankl's "Third Viennese School") children with a cyber-disorder **can go back to a smartphone**; an addicted person can't go back even to champagne, a gambler not even to the lottery.
- **His reset protocol:** start at home, not in his centre: 3–4 days, ideally a week, **with no digital technology** — parents included — and more walks and football. After 7 days: alternate days, with the curfew; finally 6 days with, 1 day without. He says he is writing it up with Manfred Spitzer and Gary Small, based on **328 inpatients**.
- The biggest obstacle isn't the child but **parents who won't come to workshops or change their own habits**.

### [48:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=2880s) Healthy habits and development

- The morning matters most: wake up with "big eyes", melatonin settling, and **low cortisol**.
- Breakfast: simple, natural protein (white cheese, milk), because protein builds brain structures; cocoa or a piece of dark chocolate for magnesium, which memory needs.
- In school: let pupils drink water during lessons and keep the windows open — oxygen is the basic fuel.
- **Coordination demonstration:** circle one arm, then the other backwards, then a leg — three simple tasks and the brain gives up. Now picture a teenager on a phone, with music, Instagram and the TV on: the brain will cope, "but it won't tell you one thing: I'll live shorter".

### [51:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=3060s) Reading, creativity and digital threats

- We adults can't stop after one episode of *Yellowstone* either, so we can't blame the children.
- **Screen-mediated relationships, lessons included, should not exceed 30% of a person's day.** Teachers: start and end each lesson without a screen; 15–20 minutes of screen in the middle.
- **Reading vs watching:** read *Lalka* and every reader's Wokulski looks different; watch the film and everyone gets the same actor. "A person who reads builds images. A person who watches photographs and doesn't switch on creativity." Otherwise "we won't have Nobel laureates".
- The digital world removed darkness: once the campfire ended, you went to sleep; now one channel ends and another begins.
- Platforms won't help, "their interest is money": TikTok asked him three times to be a consultant; he answered that he'd consult on banning it in Poland.
- Algorithm-driven feeds damage **self-esteem**: see a beautiful girl a thousand times and you forget that everyone is beautiful.
- **"The world sped up, and you have to know how to slow down."** Stoic self-limitation, Marcus Aurelius: you don't have to, but you may — only as much as works (see [[Stoicism]]).

### [54:00](https://youtube.com/watch?v=GGetWrJRCjc&t=3240s) Independence and responsibility of young people

- Back to the opening: **get them out of the house, cleverly.** After the school-leaving exam or graduation, a good dinner and the message: you can stay 30 more days, then find a job.
- His son stretched it (a bachelor's degree, then enrolled in a second university), so with the next children he said "until the end of your first degree".
- Married children: "Saturday and Sunday the wedding, Monday the after-party, Tuesday you move out." His daughter later thanked him: without that push, the 80,000 zł of wedding money would have bought a new Audi instead of part of their first flat.
- Closing line (to a participant, for a book dedication): "Remember, there's no internet in heaven."

## 🔬 Claims check

A quick web check on 2026-11-19 (a web-search agent plus my own spot check of the AI story), **not a deep-research run**. 🟢 supported · 🟡 partly supported, or the direction holds but his figure doesn't · 🔴 contradicted, or no source for a specific claim.

| # | His claim | Check | What I found |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–3 h of blue light from screens raises dopamine by 800%; a cookie by 50% | 🔴 | No source for "800%" turned up in English or Polish. The one human imaging study of screen play (Koepp 1998, PET during a video game) is usually read as roughly a doubling of striatal dopamine; stimulant drugs reach about 10×. His food figure is in the right range: lever-pressing for food raised dopamine 37% in rats (Hernandez & Hoebel 1988). Blue light's established effect is on melatonin, see [[Blue light]]. | 2 | A newborn has as many neurons as an adult, 86 billion | 🟡 | Roughly true for the neocortex (about 20 bn at birth vs 19–23 bn in adults, Larsen 2006), but 86 bn is an adult whole-brain count and most cerebellar granule cells form after birth. [^neurons] |
| 3 | No screens before 48 months | 🟡 | The direction matches the guidelines; the age is stricter than any found. WHO 2019: no screens under 1, at most 1 h a day at 2–4, "less is better". France: none before 3. The AAP's 2026 policy dropped fixed limits; its explainer says avoid screens except video chat under 18 months. [^who] [^aap] |
| 4 | The APA and WHO must add a "cyber-disorder" diagnosis | 🟡 | An opinion with a dated premise. ICD-11 already has gaming disorder (6C51) and "other specified disorders due to addictive behaviours" (6C5Y); DSM-5-TR keeps Internet Gaming Disorder in Section III, for further study. Nothing broader exists, so his point holds beyond gaming. [^icd] [^dsm] |
| 5 | Phones were withdrawn from Polish schools only recently | 🟢 | The Sejm passed the amendment on 3 Jul 2026, the Senate on 8 Jul, and the President signed it. Since 1 Sep 2026 phones are banned in primary schools and preschools, with exceptions; secondary schools set their own rules. [^ban] |
| 6 | AI: "11 agents tried to take over the internet" (*The Times*, *The Economist*); Gates: "AI will kill a billion people in 10 years"; Altman on 4 October: "they tried to break out, we shut them down" | 🔴 / 🟡 | No "11 agents" story found; it looks like several real stories run together. | 9 | 50 minutes among greenery lowers cortisol | 🟡 | The direction holds: in Hunter et al. 2019 (n=36, observational) a nature visit cut cortisol by 21.3% an hour beyond its normal daily fall, with the best return per minute at 20–30 minutes. The 50 minutes is his. [^hunter] |
| 10 | Evening screens shorten children's sleep; consolidating memory needs at least 6 hours | 🟡 | The first part holds: bedtime device use more than doubles the odds of too little sleep (meta-analysis of 20 studies, 125,198 children, OR 2.17, observational). | 11 | Women live 9 years longer | 🔴 | GUS 2024: women 82.3, men 74.9, a 7.3-year gap. Nine years was the gap around 1990. [^gus] |
| 12 | 630,000 Polish children are under professional care | 🟡 | The figure exists but means something else: NIK's 2020 estimate of children who **need** psychiatric or psychological help (≥9% of under-18s), not how many get it. [^nik] |

**Not checked:** his estimate that 25% of Polish children have cyber-disorders (he presents it as his own opinion), the 870,000 children watching Mamiko, the 328 inpatients and the paper with Spitzer and Small, the "half-size" short-term memory, and the protein, magnesium, water and oxygen advice.

**Bottom line:** the household rules mostly line up with mainstream guidance (screen curfews, no devices at bedtime, less screen time for young children, time outdoors). The neuroscience numbers used to justify them mostly don't hold up, and the AI digression mixes real events with ones I couldn't find.

## ☘️ What I take from it

- **The rules are more useful than the explanations.** The neuroscience in this talk is often shaky (see the claims check), but the household rules don't depend on it: a fixed screen curfew, phone-free meals, a daily walk, parents keeping the same rules, one voice, say it once. Most of them line up with mainstream sleep and screen-time advice anyway.
- **Parents first.** His sharpest point is that limits only work when the parents keep them too. That's the hardest part, and the cheapest.
- **Both my kids are teenagers now**, so the "first screens at 48 months" and under-13 rules are history for me; the 21:30 curfew, the 180-minute cap, phone-free dinners and evening walks are the ones that apply. The cooking-day idea fits the chores in [[Motivation system for my kids]].
- **A short reset, not a ban.** His 3–7-day no-screens reset with a gradual return is a family-sized [[Dopamine Detox]], and he frames the goal as going back to the phone in a controlled way, not abstinence.
- **His sleep figure undersells sleep.** He says memory needs at least 6 hours; the AASM recommends 8–10 hours for teenagers. That's one more reason for the 21:30 curfew.
- **Treat the figures with care.** The 800% dopamine figure, the 25% of children with cyber-disorders and the "11 AI agents" are not things to repeat.
