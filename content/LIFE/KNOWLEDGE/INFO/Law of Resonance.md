# Law of Resonance

> [!info] Context
> Parked research item (PL-246). All I had saved was the Polish phrase „prawo rezonansu”. On the Polish market it almost certainly means **Pierre Franckh's book *Prawo rezonansu***. This note covers what the "law" claims, where it comes from, and what psychology and physics actually say. Claims and evidence are kept apart.

**Evidence key:** 🟢 strong (meta-analyses / replicated) · 🟡 moderate / correlational · 🔴 untested or contradicted.

- Since 1996 he has written "how to wish" books, many with his wife Michaela Merten, e.g. *Wünsch es dir einfach – aber richtig* (2007).

  - *Chudnij z prawem rezonansu*
  - *Prawo rezonansu w miłości*
- The English edition is *The DNA Field and the Law of Resonance* (Destiny Books, 2014).

## 🧩 What the "law" claims

- **Like attracts like.** Your thoughts and especially your **feelings** "vibrate" at a frequency. Events, people and things vibrating at the same frequency are drawn to you, so you get more of what you emit.
- **The heart is a transmitter.** The heart creates a personal "resonance field". Franckh repeats the HeartMath claim that the heart's electromagnetic field is about **5,000×** stronger than the brain's, and builds on it.
- **Quantum and "DNA field" talk.** Thoughts are said to shape matter and events through the quantum field, and time is not linear.
- **The practice:** tune your "resonance" with affirmations, visualisation, gratitude and **feeling the wish as already fulfilled**. Avoid "negative resonance" such as doubt, fear or complaining.

## 🧩 Where it comes from

The law of resonance is the **New Thought "law of attraction"** under a newer, physics-sounding name:

- **Phineas Quimby** (mid-1800s): mind-cure, the root of the movement.
- **Prentice Mulford** (1886–87): thoughts as things and forces.
- **William Walker Atkinson**, ***Thought Vibration*** (1906): the "vibration" vocabulary Franckh uses.
- **Wallace Wattles** (1910), **Napoleon Hill** (1937).
- **Neville Goddard** (1940s): feel the wish as already fulfilled.
- **Rhonda Byrne, *The Secret*** (2006), and the **Abraham-Hicks** material: the mass-market wave Franckh's books rode.

The same family includes Huna prayer ([[Max Freedom Long and the Origins of Huna]]) and Braden's "feeling is the prayer" ([[Secrets of the Lost Mode of Prayer]]).

## 🔬 What physics says 🟢

- **Resonance is real physics with a narrow meaning.** A system driven at its natural frequency oscillates with a larger amplitude: a swing pushed in rhythm, a tuning fork, a bridge. No known mechanism lets thoughts "vibrate" in tune with events, people or money.
- **The heart's field is real but weak.** It is measurable by sensitive instruments, millivolts on the skin and tiny magnetic fields at a short distance. Nothing shows it carries intentions or acts on the outside world.
- **Schumann resonances** are real: electromagnetic resonances of about 7.83 Hz between the Earth and the ionosphere, driven by lightning. New Age claims about them have no support.
- **"Quantum" self-help** is rejected by physicists. Victor Stenger called it "quantum quackery"; Murray Gell-Mann called it "quantum flapdoodle".

## 🔬 What psychology says

- **No direct test exists 🔴.** I found no study testing the "law of resonance" or the law of attraction as a causal claim. As formulated, it's **unfalsifiable**. Successes confirm it, and failures are blamed on "wrong resonance". That is confirmation bias and survivorship bias built into the theory.
- **Manifestation believers take more risks 🟡** (Dixon, Hornsey & Hartley, *PSPB*, 2023–25; 3 studies, N = 1,023).
  - More than a third of people endorsed manifestation beliefs.
  - Believers *rated* themselves as more successful.
  - They were also more drawn to **risky investments**, more likely to have **gone bankrupt**, and more likely to expect quick, unlikely success.
  - The design is correlational, and there was no objective sign that manifesting works.
- **Positive fantasies backfire 🟢.**
  - Oettingen & Mayer 2002 (4 studies): **positive fantasies** about the future predicted *less* effort and *worse* results. **Positive expectations**, judging success as likely based on experience, predicted more effort and better results.
  - Kappes & Oettingen 2011: fantasising about success lowered energy, measured as a drop in systolic blood pressure, and predicted fewer goals reached.
  - "Feel it as already fulfilled" is exactly the risky kind of fantasy.
- **What works instead 🟢:**
  - **Implementation intentions**, "if X happens, then I do Y": d = 0.65 across 94 tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006). A 2024 update covering 642 tests gives d ≈ 0.27–0.66.
  - **Mental contrasting (WOOP: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan):** imagine the outcome, *then* the obstacle in yourself, then make an if-then plan. g ≈ 0.34 across 21 studies (N ≈ 15,900); publication bias is possible, so the real effect may be smaller.
- **What's true in the folk version 🟡:**
  - **Self-fulfilling prophecies** are real but usually small and don't build up much over time (Jussim & Harber 2005).
  - Mood and attention change what you **notice and pursue**. Expecting opportunities makes you look for them. Your mood also affects how people respond to you.
  - **Gratitude practice** has a small benefit over neutral activities and none over active comparison activities; see [[Gratitude]].
  - None of this needs "vibrations".

## 📒 My takeaways

1. **"Law of resonance" is the law of attraction in physics costume.** Its lineage runs from Atkinson's *Thought Vibration* (1906) to *The Secret* (2006). The physics words are borrowed, not applied.
2. **The useful kernel is attention and expectation**, and it is better captured by **WOOP plus if-then plans**: dream, then name the obstacle, then plan. Visualising success *alone* drains the energy to pursue it.
3. **Watch the money angle.** Manifestation belief goes with risk-taking and bankruptcy. That fits what I've learned about [[Investing Psychology]] and [[Cognitive Biases in Investing]]: confirmation bias plus overconfidence.
4. **Keep what's harmless and helpful:** gratitude, a positive mood, noticing opportunities. Drop the causal story.
