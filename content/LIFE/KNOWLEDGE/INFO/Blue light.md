# Blue light

## 🗒️ Description
Blue light is the high-energy visible (HEV) light emitted by screens, LEDs, and the sun. Excessive exposure, especially in the evening, suppresses melatonin production and disrupts the circadian rhythm, leading to poor sleep quality.

## 🧩 Key facts:
- Blue light wavelength: 380-500 nm
- Highest exposure sources: smartphones, monitors, LED lighting
- Evening exposure (2-3h before sleep) has the strongest negative effect on sleep
- [[Kindle]] uses e-ink which doesn't emit blue light — better for reading before bed
- Windows Night Light and similar filters reduce blue light from screens

## 👶 Screen curfews for children (added 2026-11-19)
- [[Screens and Child Development (Mariusz Jędrzejko)]] — a Polish pedagogue's age-based rule: **all screens off at 20:00 for children under 13 and at 21:30 under 18**, because what a child learned during the day is consolidated in sleep. For a child asleep by 22:00–23:00 that matches the "2–3 hours before sleep" window above.
- He also claims that 2–3 hours of blue light raises the activity of the brain's dopamine system by 800%. That's a different mechanism from the melatonin one this note describes, and I found no source for it: the one human imaging study of screen play is read as roughly a doubling of striatal dopamine, and that's about the game, not the light. See the claims check in that note.
- Evening devices and children's sleep, from the same check: in a meta-analysis of 20 studies (125,198 children), bedtime device use more than doubled the odds of getting too little sleep ([Carter et al. 2016](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/91557/)).
