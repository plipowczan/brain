# Max Freedom Long and the Origins of Huna

> [!info] Context
> Parked research item (PL-244): Max Long's history and how Huna came about. It pairs with the book note [[Mastering Your Hidden Self]] (Serge Kahili King, PL-245). This note separates **Long's own account** from **what historians found**, because the two diverge on almost every key point.

## 🗒️ Who he was

- **Max Freedom Long**: born 26 October 1890 in Sterling, Colorado; died 23 September 1971 in Vista, California.
- He was a member of the **Theosophical Society** before he went to Hawaii. This matters for everything that follows.
- He trained as a teacher at Los Angeles State Normal School (1914–16). In **1917** he went to Hawaii and taught in elementary schools on the Big Island until 1920.
- Around 1920–21 he moved to Honolulu and worked in a photo shop. He left for California around **1931–32**.
- He spent the rest of his life writing about and organising "Huna". He died by suicide while ill with bone cancer.

## 🧩 How Huna came about — Long's own story

As Long tells it in *The Secret Science Behind Miracles* (1948):

1. About four years into his time in Hawaii, he met **William Tufts Brigham**, curator of the Bishop Museum. Brigham said he had studied the kahunas (Hawaiian priests and experts) for 40 years.
2. Brigham told him to look for three things behind kahuna "magic": a directing consciousness, a force, and a substance through which the force acts.
3. Brigham died a few years later. Long kept searching, gave up in 1931, and left Hawaii.
4. One night in California, in **1934 or 1935** (sources disagree), he had his breakthrough. The secret, he concluded, was **encoded in the root meanings of Hawaiian words**, and he could decode it with a dictionary.

The result was a system with three selves, three kinds of **mana** (energy) and an invisible substance called **aka**:

| Long's term | His meaning | Dictionary meaning (Pukui-Elbert) |
|---|---|---|
| *unihipili* | low self (subconscious) | the spirit of a dead person |
| *uhane* | middle self (conscious mind) | soul, ghost |
| *aumakua* | high self (superconscious) | family or ancestral gods |
| *huna* | "the secret" — his name for the whole system | a small particle; hidden or secret; the 11th night of the lunar month; a seaweed |

## 🔬 What historians found

**The Brigham meeting almost certainly didn't happen as told.**
- Brigham lived from 1841 to 30 January 1926 and was the museum's **director from 1898 to 1918**. He was out of office before Long arrived in Honolulu around 1920–21.
- Researcher **Pali Jae Lee** searched Brigham's very detailed journals and found no mention of Long.
- Historian **Makana Risser Chai** (*Hawaiian Journal of History*, 2011) concludes the meeting is very unlikely, because Brigham's writings don't contain the stories Long credits to him.
- Chai suggests that Long's accurate details may have come from **Lahilahi Webb**, a Native Hawaiian guide at the museum.

**Huna was built from a dictionary and Western esotericism.**
- **Method:** Long mined Lorrin Andrews' 19th-century Hawaiian dictionary for "roots" inside words, a technique linguists don't accept.
- **Model of the self:** low, middle and high self map closely onto Freud's id, ego and superego, laid over a **Theosophical** base (Blavatsky).
- **Mana:** modelled on Theosophical *prana* and on Mesmerist ideas of electrical "voltages".
- **Practice:** he admired **Christian Science** and **New Thought** (e.g. Unity's *Daily Word*), and Huna prayer reads like New Thought affirmation with Hawaiian words.

**Hawaiian scholars reject it.**
- **Charles Kenn**, a Native Hawaiian scholar, was briefly a Huna Research Associate in the late 1940s.
  - In 1949 he warned that Long may have added non-Hawaiian ideas from his own Western mind.
  - Later he said outright that Huna was never Hawaiian.
- **Pali Jae Lee** (*Hoʻopono*, 1999/2007) reached the same conclusion from interviews with elders.
- No classical Hawaiian source uses "Huna" as the name of a system: not Malo, Kamakau, ʻĪʻī or Kepelino.

**Academic consensus.** Huna is a **20th-century Western esoteric/New Age system with Hawaiian labels**, not traditional Hawaiian religion. This is the view of Chai 2011, Hall 2005, Rothstein 2007 (*Handbook of New Age*, Brill) and Morgain 2016 (ANU Press).

**Scientific evidence: none.** I found no trials of Huna practices. PubMed has a single descriptive 1986 article. Claims about mana, aka threads, telepathy and healing are **untested**, not "disproven by trials".

## 🧩 How it spread

- **Books by Long:**
  - *Recovering the Ancient Magic* (Rider, London, 1936)
  - ***The Secret Science Behind Miracles*** (1948), the core text
  - *The Secret Science at Work* (1953)
  - *Growing into Light* (1955)
  - *Self-Suggestion* (1958)
  - *Psychometric Analysis* (1959)
  - *The Huna Code in Religions* (1965)
- **Organisation:** he founded **Huna Research Associates** (the Huna Fellowship) in 1945 and published the *Huna Bulletins* from 1948 until his death. **E. Otha Wingo** took over and moved the organisation to Missouri.
- **Later teachers:**
  - **Serge Kahili King**, who reworked Huna into the "Seven Principles"; see [[Mastering Your Hidden Self]]
  - **Enid Hoffman** (*Huna: A Beginner's Guide*, 1976)
  - the **Feri** neo-pagan tradition, which borrowed elements of Huna
- **In Poland:** Long's books came out through Wydawnictwo „Medium” (Warsaw), e.g. *Wiedza tajemna w praktyce* and *Dążenie ku światłu*. King's books came out through „Kos” (Katowice).

## 📒 My takeaways

1. **Huna is Long's invention, not Hawaii's.** It was assembled from Theosophy, Freud, New Thought and a dictionary, then given an origin myth (Brigham) that doesn't hold up.
2. **Its useful core is ordinary psychology.** "Talk to the low self", work with imagery and attention, forgive to clear blocks: none of it is unique to Huna, and it's better studied elsewhere. See [[Mindfulness Meditation]] for one example.
3. **Calling Huna "ancient Hawaiian wisdom" is a cultural problem as much as a factual one.** It uses the names of Hawaiian gods and ancestors to sell a Western system.
4. **To read it anyway, read it as 20th-century American esotericism.** Then it's an interesting document of its time.
