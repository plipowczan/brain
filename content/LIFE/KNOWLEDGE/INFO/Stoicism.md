# Stoicism

## 🗒️ Description

Stoicism is a practical philosophy founded in Athens by **Zeno of Citium (~300 BC)** and matured in Rome through **Seneca**, **Epictetus**, and **Marcus Aurelius**. Its claim: a good, tranquil life (*eudaimonia*) comes not from external circumstances but from how I judge and respond to them. Externals — wealth, reputation, health, other people — are *preferred* or *dispreferred*, but never the source of a flourishing life. Only my own reasoned choices are.

I already live a lot of this without naming it (see [[Trust]], [[The Tale of the Old Man and His Son's Journey]] — both are the Stoic move: refuse to label events good or bad). This note is the foundation I never formally learned.

## 🧩 Core ideas

### The dichotomy of control
The single load-bearing idea. Epictetus, *Enchiridion* §1:

> Some things are within our power, while others are not. Within our power are opinion, motivation, desire, aversion... Not within our power are our body, property, reputation, office.

- **Up to me:** my judgments, intentions, chosen actions, what I give attention to.
- **Not up to me:** outcomes, other people, the past, the body, the economy.

Anxiety comes from staking peace on things in the second column. Move your investment to the first.

### The four virtues
The Stoics held virtue as the *only* true good. Four cardinal virtues:
- **Wisdom** (*sophia*) — sound judgment about what matters.
- **Courage** (*andreia*) — acting rightly under fear or pain.
- **Justice** (*dikaiosyne*) — fairness, treating people as fellow rational beings.
- **Temperance** (*sophrosyne*) — self-discipline, moderation.

### Amor fati & living according to nature
Don't merely accept what happens — *will* it. Marcus: "Love only what happens to you and is spun with the thread of your fate." Aligning with nature means accepting the rational order of events and acting according to my own rational nature.

### Premeditatio malorum (negative visualization)
Rehearse loss in advance — what if I lost this job, this comfort, this person? Two payoffs: it dissolves anxiety (I've already met the worst in imagination) and it restores gratitude for what is still here. The opposite of toxic positivity; it's pre-loading resilience.

### The view from above
Zoom out to the scale of cities, centuries, the cosmos. The thing consuming me shrinks to proportion. A perspective tool for when a problem feels total.

### Memento mori
Remember you will die. Not morbid — clarifying. Death is the deadline that makes time scarce enough to spend well. (Connects to [[Die with Zero]] from a different angle: Stoics use mortality to act rightly *now*, Perkins to spend deliberately *now*.)

## 🛠️ Daily practices

- **Morning:** premeditatio — what's likely to go wrong today, and who I want to be when it does.
- **In the moment:** before reacting, ask "is this up to me?" If no → accept and redirect. If yes → act on virtue, not impulse. There's a gap between stimulus and response; that gap is where Stoicism lives.
- **Evening review:** Seneca's practice — replay the day, where did I act well, where did I fall short, what do I fix tomorrow. (Maps onto my [[5 Minute Journal]] evening section.)
- **Reframe judgments:** the event is neutral; my *opinion* about it does the damage. Epictetus: "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views they take of them."

## ✍️ Common misreadings

- **Stoic ≠ emotionless.** The goal isn't suppressing feeling — it's not being *ruled* by unexamined emotion. Stoics cultivated joy, gratitude, love.
- **Not passive resignation.** You act fully on what's in your control; you just don't stake your peace on the outcome. Marcus ran an empire.
- **Not toxic positivity.** It doesn't deny hardship — it trains you to meet it.

## 📖 Further reading

Start with the primary texts — short, readable, written *to be used*:
- **Marcus Aurelius — *Meditations*** — private journal of a Roman emperor practicing on himself. Best entry point. (candidate LIFE/BOOKS note)
- **Epictetus — *Enchiridion* (Handbook)** — terse, ~30 pages, the dichotomy of control distilled.
- **Seneca — *Letters from a Stoic*** — warm, practical letters on time, death, friendship, anger.
- Modern: William B. Irvine — *A Guide to the Good Life*; Ryan Holiday — *The Daily Stoic* / *The Obstacle Is the Way*.
