## 🗒️ About the author

**Serge Kahili King** is an American writer and teacher, and the best-known Huna teacher after Max Freedom Long. Anthropologist Rachel Morgain describes him as a former student of Long.

*What he says about himself (self-reported, unverified):*
- His father initiated him into Huna at 14.
- In 1957, at 18, he was adopted as a grandson by Joseph Kahili of Kauai.
- His teacher Wana Kahili gave him the title **"kahuna kupua"** in 1975, in a private ceremony on a California beach with no witnesses.
- He spent seven years in West Africa with Catholic Relief Services and says he trained in African shamanism there.

*What can be checked:*
- He lists a BA in Asian Studies (Colorado) and an MIM (Thunderbird).
- His **PhD in psychology** is from California Western University (renamed California Coast University in 1981), a for-profit distance-learning school.
- He founded **Huna International** in 1973 as a "religious order" and runs **Aloha International**.
- Other books: *Kahuna Healing* (1983), *Urban Shaman* (1990), *Earth Energies* (1992), *Instant Healing*, *Huna: Ancient Hawaiian Secrets for Modern Living* (2008).
- Anthropologist Rachel Morgain notes that his Kahili adoption claim has been disputed. No Hawaiian body certifies the "kahuna" title.

## 🚀 The Book in 3 Sentences

*(Written from the table of contents and the publisher's description, before reading.)*
King presents Huna as a rediscovered Hawaiian psychology: each person has three selves, a subconscious (**ku**), a conscious mind (**lono**) and a superconscious (**aumakua**), and life works better when they cooperate. The book teaches you to befriend the subconscious, gather and direct **mana** (life energy), clear inner blocks, reach the higher self through dreams, and use meditation with sight, sound and feeling images. It ends with "practical techniques", "creative meditation" and "spiritual integration", plus an appendix decoding the "secret code of the kahunas".

**Table of contents (1985):**
- Introduction: "The Rediscovery of Huna"
- 1 Your Three Selves · 2 Huna and Modern Psychology · 3 What Your Subconscious Is Really Like · 4 Your Conscious Mind · 5 Getting to Know Your Subconscious
- 6 The Reality of the Invisible · 7 Mana, Mysterious Energy of Life · 8 Clearing the Path to Power · 9 The Road to Self-Mastery
- 10 Your Superconscious Self · 11 Dream Talk · 12 Practical Techniques · 13 Creative Meditation · 14 Spiritual Integration
- Appendix: "The Secret Code of the Kahunas"

## 🧩 King's Huna in a nutshell — what it claims

**The three selves.**
- Long called them *unihipili / uhane / aumakua*: low, middle and high self.
- In this book King uses *ku / lono / aumakua* and calls Long's "low/middle/high" labels misleading.
- In later books the higher self becomes **Kane**.

**The Seven Principles.** These are King's signature list. As far as I could find, it's documented in *Urban Shaman* (1990). I couldn't confirm that it appears as a list in this book.

| Word | King's principle | Dictionary meaning (Pukui-Elbert) |
|---|---|---|
| **IKE** | The world is what you think it is | to see, to know |
| **KALA** | There are no limits | to loosen, to free |
| **MAKIA** | Energy flows where attention goes | aim, purpose |
| **MANAWA** | Now is the moment of power | time, season |
| **ALOHA** | To love is to be happy with | love, compassion |
| **MANA** | All power comes from within | divine or supernatural power |
| **PONO** | Effectiveness is the measure of truth | goodness, correctness |

The gap between the two right-hand columns is the whole story: King's principles are modern self-help ideas hung on Hawaiian words.

**Mana as energy.**
- King treated mana as a measurable life energy, with "manaplates" and influences from Wilhelm Reich.
- In 1992 he partly retracted this, admitting the Hawaiian word means divine power or authority.

**Origin myth.** According to Wikipedia's reading of the book's introduction, King traces Huna to "star people" from the **Pleiades**, by way of Mu/Lemuria.

## 🔬 What scholars and evidence say

- **Not traditional Hawaiian religion.** Huna is a 20th-century Western esoteric system with Hawaiian labels. This is the consensus of Chai (2011), Hall (2005), Rothstein (2007) and Morgain (2016); details in [[Max Freedom Long and the Origins of Huna]].
- **A Native Hawaiian scholar's view.** Lisa Kahaleole Hall (*The Contemporary Pacific*, 2005) writes that this kind of Huna resembles no Hawaiian worldview. She singles out King's claimed lineage from the Pleiades.
- **Misused god names.** Scholars of religion (Rothstein, Chai) note that Kū, Lono and Kāne are the names of major Hawaiian **gods**, not "selves". Using them this way is appropriation.
- **No scientific testing.** PubMed has no trials of Huna; the only hit is a descriptive 1986 article in the *Hawaii Medical Journal*.
- **What maps onto real psychology** (my reading, not a validation of Huna):
  - The split into a fast, emotional, habit-driven "subconscious" and a deliberate conscious mind resembles the dual-process models in psychology.
  - "Energy flows where attention goes" restates the plain fact that attention shapes behaviour.
  - Imagery and meditation practices have their own modest evidence base; see [[Mindfulness Meditation]].
  - None of this supports mana as an energy, superconscious selves or Pleiadian origins.
