# 🚀 The Book in 3 Sentences
Book is about different techniques how to learn fast and efficient

In more detail: Kotarski, a Polish science popularizer, explains why the way schools teach us to study (re-reading, cramming) doesn't work. He then describes about a dozen study methods from cognitive psychology, each with a memorable Polish nickname. The core message: test yourself instead of re-reading, and spread your study over several sessions. It covers much of the same science as [[Learn Like a Pro]], in Polish and for a Polish reader.

> [!note] What this note is based on
> I read the book in 2020 and kept only a one-line note. The summary below comes from the publisher, interviews with the author and published reviews (links in Resources), not from my own reading notes. An earlier version of this note claimed the book debunks "learning styles" and covers mind maps; I couldn't confirm either, so those claims are gone.

# 🎨 Impressions

## Who Should Read It?
Polish-speaking students, parents and anyone who learned to study by re-reading and highlighting. It's the most accessible Polish introduction to evidence-based learning. If you already know the research, or have read [[Learn Like a Pro]], expect little new. Reviewers say the same.

# 📒 Summary + Notes

## How the book is built
- **A long opening on school.** The first ~70 pages tell how the school system came to be and why it trains us to learn badly. The methods start only after that. Several reviewers found this part too long.
- **About 13 methods with nicknames.** Each technique gets a catchy Polish name, so it's easy to remember and talk about.
- **Research-based.** Kotarski worked on the book for about two years. He consulted John Dunlosky and Katherine Rawson of Kent State University, two of the main researchers on study techniques. He says he relied mainly on cognitive-psychology research.

## Key ideas (confirmed from interviews and reviews)
1. **Re-reading doesn't work.** It feels productive because the text becomes familiar, but familiarity is not memory.
2. **Test yourself, even before you know the answer.** The *metoda testu zderzeniowego* ("crash-test method") is retrieval practice. Trying to recall something strengthens memory, and even a failed attempt helps you learn the answer afterwards.
3. **Flashcards** (*metoda fiszek*) — question on one side, answer on the other, as a tool for self-testing.
4. **Spread the repetitions out.** Go back to the material in several sessions, and use a different method each time instead of repeating the same one.
5. **Connect information to emotions and to what you already know.** The book covers mnemonics and *powtarzanie elaboracyjne* (elaborative rehearsal: explaining new material through things you already understand).
6. **Memory palace** — the method of placing images in a familiar place, covered briefly (one reviewer says too briefly).

## How it fits with the other notes
- The two methods the book leans on most are the two rated "high utility" in the 2013 review by Dunlosky (whom Kotarski consulted) and colleagues: **practice testing** and **distributed practice**. Both are explained in depth in [[Spaced Repetition and Flashcards from Your Own Notes]].
- Elaboration in your own words is the same mechanism behind good permanent notes in a [[Zettelkasten]] and the recite step of [[Cornel Notes Taking System]].

## ⚖️ Criticism
- No expert criticism of the science that I could find. The complaints are about form: a long introduction, and "nothing new" for readers who know the research.
- Kotarski calls himself a popularizer, not a researcher. The book translates research; it doesn't add to it.
- At least one review online was a paid partnership with the publisher, so take glowing reviews with a pinch of salt.
