## 🗒️ About the author

- **Gregg Braden** is an American New Age author and speaker, published by Hay House.
- **His self-reported career**, which I couldn't confirm independently:
  - a BSc in earth sciences (Metropolitan State College, Denver);
  - computer systems geologist at Phillips Petroleum (1982–85);
  - senior computer systems designer at Martin Marietta Aerospace (1985–89);
  - first technical operations manager at Cisco (1990–91);
  - writing full time since 1991.
- **Main books:** *The Isaiah Effect* (2000), *The God Code* (2004), *The Divine Matrix* (2007), *The Spontaneous Healing of Belief* (2008), *Fractal Time* (2011), *Pure Human* (2025).
- **His recurring theme:** ancient traditions encoded a "lost" science, and modern physics and biology confirm it. Critics dispute the "confirm" part; see below.

## 🚀 The Book in 3 Sentences

*(Written from summaries and the publisher's description, before reading.)*
Braden argues that a wordless, **feeling-based** way of praying was lost to the West about 1,700 years ago through Church edits of scripture, but survived in Tibetan monasteries and in Native American rites. The core teaching: **feeling is the prayer**. Words and chants only create the feeling, so you pray by feeling as if the prayer is already answered. Around that core he works through four "keys": **hurt** as a teacher whose lesson is **wisdom**, **blessing** as a way to release hurt without condoning it, and **beauty** as a transforming force.

## 🧩 Main claims

- **The Tibetan abbot story.** In the 1990s an abbot told Braden that feeling is the prayer, and that the monks' chanting is only a way to create the feeling. This anchors the book.
- **The "original" Gospel saying.** Braden offers a reading of John 16:23–24 along the lines of "ask without hidden motive and be surrounded by your answer". He presents it as the lost original of "ask and you shall receive". It comes from Neil Douglas-Klotz's *Prayers of the Cosmos*, which is an **interpretive rendering**, not a translation of a manuscript.
- **Edited-out teachings.** Braden says the Church removed this mode of prayer from scripture in the 4th century, and that the Essenes and other traditions kept it.
- **Blessing releases hurt.** Blessing whoever or whatever hurt you is said to "release" the hurt, so you can move on without condoning anything.
- **Beauty and the "field".** Seeing beauty changes the one who sees it. Feelings act on a unified "field" that connects everything, a theme from his *Divine Matrix*.
- **The "science" he usually cites** in support:
  - the 1993 Washington DC meditation experiment;
  - the "phantom DNA effect";
  - HeartMath's DNA experiments;
  - Backster's experiments with cells reacting to emotions.

## 🔬 What the evidence says

**Prayer acting on the world: no effect 🟢.**
- **STEP trial** (Benson et al. 2006, *American Heart Journal*): 1,802 bypass-surgery patients in 6 hospitals.
  - Intercessory prayer made no difference: complications were 52% with prayer and 51% without.
  - Patients told *for certain* that they were being prayed for had **more** complications (59%).
- **Cochrane review** (Roberts et al. 2009): 10 trials, 7,646 patients. No significant effect on death, clinical state or readmission. The authors doubted that more trials were worthwhile.
- These trials tested other people praying for patients, not Braden's own "feeling" mode. But his book makes the same kind of claim about effects on the outside world.

**The studies he leans on are weak 🔴.**
- **The 1993 Washington DC experiment** (Hagelin et al. 1999). Some 4,000 meditators were said to have cut violent crime.
  - Physicist Robert Park called it a "clinic in data distortion".
  - Every member of the "independent" review board practised Transcendental Meditation.
  - It earned the **1994 Ig Nobel Peace Prize**.
- **"Phantom DNA"** (Poponin/Gariaev, 1990s) has no confirmed peer-reviewed publication and no replication.
- **HeartMath DNA experiments** appeared only in conference proceedings and HeartMath's own publication series, with no independent replication.

**Scholarship.**
- **There is no Aramaic original of John to recover.** The Gospel was written in Greek, and the Syriac (Aramaic) Peshitta was translated *from* Greek, probably in the early 5th century. A "lost Aramaic meaning" of John 16:24 is a modern devotional rendering, not recovered text.
- **Reviewers.** *Publishers Weekly* called the lost mode of prayer in *The Isaiah Effect* "somewhat garbled", and called *Pure Human* a "pseudoscientific treatise".
- **Misreading science.** Biologist PZ Myers shows Braden misdating and overreading J. Andrew Armour's research on the heart's nerve cells (the "heart brain").

**What psychology does support: effects on the person praying, not on events 🟡.**
- **Gratitude:** small benefit over neutral activities (meta-analysis d ≈ 0.17) and none over active alternatives. See [[Gratitude]].
- **Positive religious coping:** goes with better adjustment to stress, while negative religious coping goes with worse (meta-analysis of 49 studies).
- **Loving-kindness meditation:** increases positive emotions, which build personal resources over weeks (Fredrickson et al. 2008). This is the closest scientific cousin of Braden's "blessing".
- **One caution:** "feel it as already answered" is close to the **positive fantasies** that drain motivation in Oettingen's research. See [[Law of Resonance]].
