## Details

### 1. Fast-day protocol (during the 24h)

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** During the fast, the only levers are hydration, electrolytes, black coffee/tea, and levothyroxine timing. Getting these right keeps the weekly 24h fast tolerable and — critically — keeps levothyroxine absorption consistent, which is condition #3 for continuing to fast in the companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] (fasting section). Mechanisms (fT3/rT3 shift, iodine, levo interactions) are already covered there; this is the practice layer.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** On a true water fast there is no food; electrolytes come from: a pinch of unrefined salt (Polish sol klodawska / Himalayan) in water or coffee for sodium; potassium from a 'lite salt' (KCl) or 1/8 tsp cream of tartar (~250 mg K) dissolved in water; magnesium from a citrate/glycinate capsule. A highly-mineralized still mineral water (e.g. Muszynianka, Staropolanka — high Mg/bicarbonate) can partly substitute for salt/mag pills. Avoid any 'thyroid support' electrolyte product containing kelp/mega-iodine (companion iodine rule).
- **Prep / recipe:** DIY fast-day electrolyte drink: 1-1.5 L water + ~1/2-3/4 tsp salt + 1/8 tsp potassium (lite salt or cream of tartar) + 200-300 mg magnesium citrate; add a squeeze of lemon (negligible calories) for taste. Mix once in the morning and sip through the day rather than downing it in one go. Black coffee and plain tea alongside, no milk/sugar/sweetener-with-calories.
- **Template line:** Levo on waking with water; wait 30-60 min, then black coffee (no milk/sugar). Sip 1-1.5 L water with ~1/4 tsp salt + a little potassium + 300 mg magnesium across the day.

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Potassium: never take as one large bolus — FDA links oral KCl doses >99 mg to small-bowel lesions; hyperkalemia risk is real if you have kidney impairment or take ACE inhibitors / ARBs / potassium-sparing diuretics (check with a clinician first). Sodium: go easy if hypertensive. Magnesium: excess citrate causes loose stools. Red flags to break the fast immediately: heart palpitations, severe dizziness/fainting, confusion, chest pain, or a hypoglycemia crash (shaky/sweaty; glucose <54 mg/dL).
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Coffee x levothyroxine is the key antagonism on a fast day: coffee cuts levo absorption ~29-36% (2008 espresso crossover trial: T4 dropped 36% when taken within 10 min) — separate by 30-60 min. Calcium and iron block levo too — separate ~4 h (not usually an issue while fasting, but relevant for any morning supplement). Take magnesium away from the levo dose (divalent cations can bind levo). Sodium and potassium should be kept in rough balance; sipping both, not just salt, prevents cramps.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** The one Hashimoto-specific requirement during the fast is levothyroxine consistency (companion condition #3): same empty-stomach routine every fast day, water only, ≥30-60 min before coffee. If coffee timing is hard to hold, a liquid or soft-gel levothyroxine formulation is far less coffee-sensitive (Endocrine Society 2022) and worth asking the endo about. Do not use kelp/iodine-loaded electrolyte or 'thyroid' products to 'remineralize' — excess iodine is the clearest avoid in the companion note.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** For a single well-hydrated 24h fast, plain water plus a little salt is usually enough; potassium and magnesium supplements are optional and mainly earn their place if you get cramps, headaches, or poor sleep. A highly-mineralized mineral water can replace the pills. Reserve full commercial electrolyte powders for repeated or multi-day fasts. Levothyroxine, by contrast, is a must-take on schedule regardless.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** This IS the fast-day protocol. Allowed during the 24h (no meaningful calories/insulin): water, black coffee, plain tea, and sugar-free electrolytes. Breaks the fast (save for the refeed): milk/cream in coffee, any caloric sweetener, bone broth, BCAAs/EAAs, gummy vitamins. Levothyroxine does NOT break the fast (no calories) — take it on waking on an empty stomach, water only, wait 30-60 min before coffee; if you fast overnight-into-day, this timing is easy to hold. Sip electrolytes across the day, never as one bolus. Refeed handling is a separate item.
- **Evidence:** Levo-coffee interaction: strong (multiple crossover studies + systematic review) — 🟢. Black coffee not breaking a fast: strong/moderate (small metabolic studies + calorie logic) — 🟢. Specific electrolyte gram targets: weak/moderate — 🟡/🔴 (fasting-community consensus and physiology, mostly derived from multi-day fasts, no RCT for a weekly 24h fast; likely conservative to over-generous for 24h). Generalizability: electrolyte and coffee data are general-population, not Hashimoto- or sex-specific; the levo-timing evidence applies directly to the male reader on levothyroxine. Cross-link: [[Hashimoto and Diet]] fasting + levothyroxine-timing sections.

### 2. Breaking the fast (refeed)

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** After a 24h fast insulin sensitivity is high, so a carb bolus overshoots into a glucose spike then a reactive-hypoglycemia crash — the opposite of what you want with Hashimoto's frequent metabolic/insulin-resistance overlap. A gentle, glucose-stable refeed keeps the fast a net positive and eases digestion. True refeeding syndrome is not a real risk at 24h (mechanisms and the fT3/rT3 shift are in the companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]]); this is the practice layer, and links to [[Glucose Spikes and Performance]].
- **Targets / numbers:** Break with a small first food ~150-400 kcal delivering 20-30 g protein, then a full balanced meal ~30-90 min later. Use carbohydrate-last ordering: protein + fat + non-starchy veg first, concentrated starch/sugar 10+ min later (RCTs show ~17-37% lower post-meal glucose peaks). No multi-day calorie titration is needed — refeeding syndrome only becomes a clinical threshold after roughly 5-7+ days of starvation, not a 24h fast. Optionally take electrolytes (Na ~1 g, K ~400 mg, Mg ~120 mg) 30-60 min before the first bite.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Gentle, protein/fat-forward, low-glycemic first foods (Poland-available): natural kefir or plain yogurt (also fermented -> gut/immune), maslanka, 2-3 eggs, warm rosol / bone broth (rehydrates + sodium/potassium + collagen), avocado, cooked non-starchy vegetables (spinach, courgette, green beans), a little olive oil, white fish. Fermented sides — kiszona kapusta (sauerkraut), kiszone ogorki, kimchi — fit the Mediterranean/gut angle. Save bread, rice, potatoes, and fruit/juice for last or the next meal.
- **Prep / recipe:** Start with a mug of warm rosol (bone broth) with a pinch of salt, or a glass of kefir. Wait ~10-15 min, then 2-3 eggs soft-scrambled in olive oil + a handful of sauerkraut + wilted spinach. If you want carbs, add a small portion of buckwheat or rye bread at the END of that plate, not the start. Keep the first meal modest; resume normal-sized meals only after your gut has settled.
- **Template line:** Start: 1 glass kefir or a mug of rosol. Then: 2-3 eggs + sauerkraut + cooked greens in olive oil. Any carbs (rice/bread/fruit) come last, if at all.

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Don't break the fast with a carb/sugar bolus (fruit juice, pastries, a big pasta plate) — that produces the largest post-fast glucose spike and a reactive-hypoglycemia crash an hour later. Don't overeat volume on the first plate (nausea, bloating, cramping). Red flags: if you feel shaky, sweaty, or get palpitations while still fasting, break gently but promptly with protein + fat + a small amount of carbohydrate (not sugar alone). Muscle weakness, confusion, or swelling after eating are refeeding-syndrome signs — only plausible after multi-day fasts, and warrant medical care.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Protein + fat + fibre before carbohydrate is the core synergy: it slows gastric emptying and stimulates GLP-1, blunting the glucose rise (carbs-last RCTs). A vinegar drink or a salad first blunts it further. Antagonist to watch: levothyroxine must already have been taken earlier on an empty stomach (>=30-60 min before this first food) — the refeed is 'food', so it ends the levo absorption window; and any calcium or iron in the meal should sit ~4 h from the levo dose.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Post-fast insulin sensitivity plus the insulin-resistance that often accompanies Hashimoto's means a carb-first refeed overshoots more than in a metabolically healthy person — carbs-last matters more here. Fermented first foods (kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi) support gut and immune regulation in line with the companion note's Mediterranean/whole-food default, but keep iodine moderate: don't 'remineralize' with kelp, seaweed salad, or mega-iodine broth powders.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** Whole food fully covers breaking a 24h fast — no supplement is required. Electrolytes taken during the fast can simply be sipped just before eating. A real-food protein source (eggs, kefir, fish) is the better gentle first food than a shake; a whey or casein shake is an acceptable convenience option but is less soothing to the gut. Save any starchy carbohydrate or fruit for the end of the meal or the following meal.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** This IS the refeed sequence and sits directly after the fast-day protocol. Order: (1) electrolytes/water 30-60 min before eating; (2) a small protein+fat first food — kefir, broth, or eggs; (3) wait ~10-15 min; (4) a full Mediterranean-style plate built veg/protein/fat-first with any starch last; (5) resume normal eating across the remaining window. Do NOT compress the whole day's calories into a single post-fast bolus. Levothyroxine should already have been taken earlier, fasted; it does not enter the refeed. Guardrails for whether to fast at all are a separate item.
- **Evidence:** Carbohydrate-last / food-order glucose benefit: strong-to-moderate (multiple RCTs in prediabetes, T2D, and gestational diabetes; systematic review rates carbs-last lowering glucose/insulin at moderate certainty) — 🟢/🟡. Refeeding-syndrome-negligible-at-24h: strong (clinical threshold is multi-day starvation) — 🟢. 'Gentle first food' specifics (broth/kefir/eggs order): weak — 🔴 (fasting-community consensus and physiology, not trial-tested for a 24h refeed). Generalizability: food-order studies are in dysglycemic cohorts, transferring well to a male with metabolic-risk overlap, but none are Hashimoto-specific. Cross-link: [[Hashimoto and Diet]], [[Glucose Spikes and Performance]].

### 3. Fasting-safety guardrails (Hashimoto-specific)

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** The companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] already establishes that a 24h fast measurably nudges thyroid hormones (fT3 down ~6%, reverse-T3 up ~16%) and is 'not shown to harm but long-term autoimmune effect unstudied.' This item is the practical guardrail layer that keeps the weekly fast net-neutral for a hypothyroid, levothyroxine-treated reader: how often, when NOT to fast, what to monitor, and when to abort. Do not re-derive the mechanism — this operationalizes it.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Not food-centric, but nutrient sufficiency on eating days buffers the fast: hit protein and the companion note's Se/Zn/Fe/D/B12/omega-3 targets so the fast is layered on a well-nourished baseline, not a chronic deficit. Enter the fast well-hydrated and after a balanced prior meal (protein + fat + fibrous veg + a little slow carbohydrate) to blunt the overnight glucose drop. Don't schedule the fast on a day of hard training, illness, or short sleep.
- **Prep / recipe:** Pre-fast 'anchor' meal template to start the fast glucose-stable: a protein + healthy-fat + fibrous-veg plate with modest slow carbohydrate — e.g. baked salmon + olive oil + roasted vegetables + a small portion of buckwheat, plus plenty of water. This flattens the entry into the fast and reduces early hunger/hypoglycemia the next morning.
- **Template line:** One 24h fast per week, maximum. Skip it during a flare, illness, high-stress, poor-sleep, or hard-training week.

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Abort signals (break the fast now): a hypoglycemia crash (shaky, sweaty, confused, palpitations; glucose <54 mg/dL), chest pain or an irregular/pounding heartbeat, severe dizziness or fainting, and worsening Hashimoto symptoms during or after fasts (deepening fatigue, cold intolerance, brain fog, new hair shedding — the latter also prompts a ferritin/zinc check per other items). Ceiling: never exceed 1x24h/week; do not fast during a flare, acute illness, or a high-stress / low-sleep stretch. If TSH creeps up or symptoms worsen across cycles, cut frequency or stop and consult the endocrinologist.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Hypothyroid metabolism is already down-regulated, so a fast can amplify fatigue and cold intolerance more than in euthyroid people, and unstable blood sugar plus adrenal stress can, in principle, aggravate autoimmune flares. Note a generalizability caveat: much of the fasting-and-thyroid caution literature is female-skewed (women's HPT/HPA axes appear more fasting-sensitive), so the male reader is likely somewhat less reactive — but the guardrails still apply, and the long-term autoimmune effect remains unstudied (companion note).
- **Food-first vs supplement:** This guardrail is behavioral, not a supplement decision. The 'food-first' answer when fasting reliably triggers symptoms is to shorten or skip the fast rather than supplement around it. Ensure eating-day nutrient sufficiency (companion note's micronutrient targets) and adequate protein BEFORE layering fasting stress on top; no pill offsets fasting into a flare or a high-stress week.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** This IS the guardrail layer wrapping the weekly fast. Rules: one 24h fast/week, ~24h max; skip it during any flare, illness, high-stress, poor-sleep, or hard-training period; monitor TSH/fT4 across cycles (companion); abort on hypoglycemia, palpitations, or worsening thyroid symptoms; keep levothyroxine consistent (fast-day protocol item) and refeed gently (refeed item). Per the companion note, treat the weekly fast as a personal experiment steered by lab feedback and symptoms, not a settled-safe habit — and reconsider frequency if labs or symptoms drift.
- **Evidence:** Cortisol -> reverse-T3 / caloric-restriction -> low-T3 mechanism: moderate — 🟡 (established non-thyroidal-illness physiology; but magnitude for a single weekly 24h fast is modest and reverses on refeed, per the companion note's fT3 -6% / rT3 +16%). 'Cap 1/week, don't fast during flares, defined abort signals, monitor across cycles': weak — 🔴 (clinical/expert-consensus and prudential, no Hashimoto RCT). Generalizability: fasting-thyroid caution data skew female and healthy-cohort; male + Hashimoto specifically is unstudied (companion note). Cross-link explicitly: [[Hashimoto and Diet]] fasting section.

### 4. Protein targets

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Muscle preservation is the top priority on a repeated weekly 24h-fast pattern. Hypothyroid muscle is already prone to atrophy and anabolic resistance (T3 governs muscle homeostasis, myogenesis and mitochondrial function), and a skipped-day plus compressed eating window adds catabolic pressure, so dietary protein is the single biggest lever to hold lean mass while fat drops. See companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] for the fasting -> thyroid-hormone shift (fT3 down ~6%, rT3 up ~16% on a 24h fast).
- **Targets / numbers:** 1.6-2.2 g protein per kg bodyweight per day for muscle preservation in an energy deficit / fasting pattern (~1.6 g/kg is the meta-analytic minimum for maximizing muscle retention; ~2.2 g/kg the practical upper end). During aggressive fat-loss phases the evidence supports up to 2.3-3.1 g/kg of FAT-FREE mass. Worked example for an ~80 kg male: ~130-175 g/day. Per meal: ~0.4 g/kg (~30-40 g high-quality protein) to clear the leucine threshold (~2.5-3 g leucine); the anabolic-resistant / over-60 end needs the higher per-meal amount. Spread across 3-4 protein feedings inside the eating window rather than one bolus.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Poland-available, everyday: chicken breast (~31 g protein / 100 g cooked; 150 g ~46 g), lean beef/pork (~26-30 g / 100 g), eggs (~6-7 g each), skyr / Greek yogurt (Piatnica skyr ~11 g / 100 g; 200 g ~22 g), twarog / curd cheese (~18 g / 100 g; 200 g ~36 g), Baltic herring / mackerel / cod / salmon (~20-25 g / 100 g), lentils and beans (~9 g / 100 g cooked, incomplete -> pair with grain), whey isolate (~24 g / 30 g scoop). Realistic single portions land 20-46 g each.
- **Prep / recipe:** Batch-cook a protein anchor once or twice a week: poach or roast 4-6 chicken thighs/breasts, hard-boil a tray of eggs, keep skyr and twarog on hand for zero-cook protein. Add a whey isolate scoop to water/kefir as the fastest way to hit ~25 g. Pair with lemon, herbs, and vegetables; combine plant proteins (legume + grain) to complete the amino profile.
- **Template line:** 150 g chicken breast (~46 g protein) OR 200 g skyr + 1 whey scoop (~46 g protein)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** 1.6-2.2 g/kg (and up to ~3.1 g/kg FFM in cuts) is safe for people with healthy kidneys; get eGFR first if any renal impairment and moderate intake accordingly. Very high protein is not a substitute for total calories. Red flags that mean investigate, not push more protein: ongoing lean-mass loss, weakness or proximal-muscle fatigue despite hitting protein -> check that levothyroxine is adequately dosed (undertreatment itself drives myopathy/atrophy) and check ferritin and zinc. Do not swap whole-food protein for isolated BCAA/leucine mega-doses.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Biggest synergy: protein + resistance training preserves muscle far better than either alone. Leucine is the MPS trigger — distribute protein (per-meal threshold) instead of one large feed. Adequate energy and carbohydrate spare protein from being burned. Antagonists vs the pill: dairy (calcium/casein) and soy protein at the levothyroxine dose block its absorption — take levothyroxine on an empty stomach and keep protein-containing meals 30-60 min away (calcium/iron-rich foods ~4 h). A morning protein shake or milky coffee right at wake-up clashes with levo timing.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Undertreated / hypothyroid states cause anabolic resistance and proximal myopathy, so hitting the protein target matters MORE in Hashimoto's, not less — but protein cannot compensate for an under-dosed levothyroxine (fix the dose with the endo). Dairy and soy are common protein sources yet interfere with levothyroxine absorption, so schedule them away from the pill. No evidence that protein intake affects thyroid antibodies (see [[Hashimoto and Diet]] — micronutrient/diet -> antibody links are weak).
- **Food-first vs supplement:** Food-first easily hits 1.6-2.2 g/kg for a meat/fish/dairy eater. A whey or casein powder is a convenience tool, not a requirement — most useful (a) to break the fast fast with ~25 g leucine-rich protein, and (b) to reach the ~0.4 g/kg per-meal threshold inside a short eating window. Plant-heavy eaters may need a scoop or careful legume+grain pairing to close the gap and complete amino profiles.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** On the 24h fast day protein intake is essentially zero; a short fast transiently pauses muscle protein synthesis but does NOT impair it long-term if you compensate on eating days (2024 randomized TRE/energy-restriction data show MPS rates preserved with adequate protein). Break the fast PROTEIN-FORWARD: 25-40 g of high-quality, leucine-rich protein first (whey, eggs, skyr/kefir) for satiety, glucose stability, and to restart MPS (see item 'Breaking the fast'). Then distribute the day's remaining protein across 3-4 feeds of ~0.4 g/kg through the compressed eating window, front-loading earlier. Because one full day is skipped weekly, aim the TOP of the range (~2.0-2.2 g/kg) on eating days so the weekly average stays high.
- **Evidence:** Strong for 1.6-2.2 g/kg preserving lean mass during energy restriction — multiple RCTs, meta-analyses, and the ISSN position stand converge here. Moderate-to-strong that intermittent fasting / TRE does not impair muscle protein synthesis WHEN protein is adequate (2024 controlled dietary intervention). Generalizability caveat: most MPS and leucine-threshold data come from young men and older adults, and per-meal thresholds derive from resistance-training studies — the male reader fits these cohorts well. Thyroid-SPECIFIC protein-dose RCTs are lacking (weak); the thyroid rationale is mechanistic (T3-muscle axis, sarcopenia prevention) rather than trial-proven.

### 5. Selenium (+ myo-inositol combo)

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Selenium is the thyroid's built-in antioxidant cofactor: selenoprotein glutathione peroxidases quench the hydrogen peroxide generated during iodine organification, and the deiodinases that make T3 from T4 are selenoproteins. In Hashimoto's it modestly lowers TPO antibodies. Pairing it with myo-inositol (a second messenger for TSH signaling) adds TSH normalization in subclinical hypothyroidism. See [[Hashimoto and Diet]] for the antibody-effect nuance (surrogate marker, effect wanes by 12 months, ETA does not recommend it routinely).

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Brazil nuts — richest but WILDLY variable (~68-91 ug per nut on average, but a single nut can range from ~10 to >200 ug depending on soil/region), selenium bound mostly as selenomethionine. So 1-2 nuts/day usually cover the RDA but the dose is unreliable. Consistent everyday sources (Poland): sea fish — cod, Baltic herring, sardines, tuna (~25-45 ug / 100 g), eggs (~15 ug each), pork/beef/poultry, sunflower seeds, whole-grain rye bread. Two fish meals a week plus eggs comfortably cover needs.
- **Prep / recipe:** Eat 1-2 Brazil nuts with breakfast as a fixed habit (do NOT graze a whole handful — selenosis risk). Roasting is fine; selenomethionine is heat-stable. Get the reliable baseline from fish 2x/week and daily eggs rather than trusting nut-to-nut selenium.
- **Template line:** 1-2 Brazil nuts OR 120 g baked cod

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Selenium UL = 400 ug/day (food + supplement combined). Selenosis red flags -> STOP: garlic/metallic breath, brittle hair and nails or hair loss, GI upset, skin rash, fatigue, peripheral neuropathy. Over-reliance on Brazil nuts can silently blow past the UL (a few high-selenium nuts daily). Never stack a 200 ug selenomethionine pill with a Brazil-nut habit. Emerging caution: chronically high selenium status has been associated with higher type-2-diabetes risk — don't mega-dose long term.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Selenium x iodine — the cofactor rule (protective): correcting or pushing iodine in a selenium-deficient thyroid can WORSEN thyrocyte injury, because without adequate selenium/GPx the peroxide from iodine handling isn't neutralized. Practical rule: never push iodine on a selenium-deficient thyroid; ensure selenium adequacy first. Selenium x zinc: both are needed for deiodinase-driven T4->T3 conversion (synergy). Myo-inositol synergy: MI+Se lowers TSH and TgAb more than selenium alone. Selenium does not block levothyroxine, but if taken as a multi-mineral with calcium/iron, keep that ~4 h from the pill.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Autoimmune-specific: selenium's documented Hashimoto effect is lowering TPOAb at 3 and 6 months (pooled SMD ~ -0.96) in treated and untreated patients, but the effect is GONE by 12 months and GRADE certainty is low — ETA does NOT recommend routine selenium; reserve for documented deficiency (see [[Hashimoto and Diet]]). The selenium-iodine interaction is the key autoimmune caveat for this reader: adequate selenium protects the gland while it handles dietary iodine. The myo-inositol combo is specifically studied in Hashimoto's with SUBCLINICAL hypothyroidism (TSH ~3-6, positive antibodies), not overt disease.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** Food-first (fish, eggs, 1-2 Brazil nuts) unless there is (a) documented selenium deficiency, or (b) a deliberate 3-6 month antibody-lowering trial agreed with the endocrinologist. If supplementing, a 200 ug selenomethionine pill gives consistent dosing that unreliable Brazil nuts cannot. Reserve the myo-inositol + selenium combination pill for subclinical hypothyroidism under clinician guidance — it is a targeted intervention, not a daily staple.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** Selenium and myo-inositol are not time-critical to the fast window — take them on EATING days with food (better GI tolerance and steady absorption). On the fast day, skip Brazil nuts and any food selenium (they carry calories/fat and break the fast); pills of pure selenomethionine + myo-inositol are essentially calorie-free but purists still take them on eating days for routine consistency. Myo-inositol (600 mg) is a negligible-calorie sugar alcohol — it won't meaningfully break a fast, but there's no reason to dose it fasted. Keep any mineral-containing supplement away from the levothyroxine window.
- **Evidence:** Selenium antibody-lowering: strong that it lowers TPOAb short-term / moderate-to-weak that it helps clinically (surrogate marker, effect gone by 12 months, low GRADE, ETA neutral) — carried verbatim from [[Hashimoto and Diet]]. Myo-inositol + selenium for subclinical hypothyroidism: moderate — a 2026 systematic review + meta-analysis with trial-sequential analysis (3 RCTs, ~288 patients) found significantly lower TSH and TgAb vs selenium alone, but NO significant change in TPOAb, T3, or T4; trials are few and small, interpret with caution. Se-iodine cofactor rule: strong mechanistic + observational. Generalizability: MI+Se trials are small and mixed-sex; no male-specific data, but the mechanism is sex-neutral.

### 6. Zinc

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Zinc is a cofactor for the iodothyronine deiodinases that convert T4 to the active T3, and it is central to immune regulation. Deficiency blunts T4->T3 conversion, lowers T3/fT4, and weakens TSH signaling — and there is a vicious circle because thyroid hormone is itself needed to absorb zinc. Practically it is a high-yield lever for a fasting eater whose intake window is compressed. See [[Hashimoto and Diet]]: frame zinc as support for T3 conversion, energy, hair and immune function, NOT as an antibody cure (micronutrient -> antibody links are weak).
- **Targets / numbers:** RDA: 11 mg/day for adult men (8 mg women). Typical supplemental dose for repletion: 15-30 mg elemental zinc (picolinate or glycinate are well absorbed). Tolerable upper intake level (UL): 40 mg/day from all sources. If supplementing above ~25 mg for more than a few weeks, add ~1-2 mg copper. Aim a dietary zinc:copper ratio around 8-15:1.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Oysters — by far the richest (~30-75 mg / 100 g; a handful covers days of need). Red meat: beef (~5-8 mg / 3 oz), pork, lamb. Pumpkin seeds / pestki dyni (~2.2 mg / 30 g, ~7-8 mg / 100 g). Hemp seeds (~3 mg / 30 g). Cheese, eggs, cashews. Legumes (lentils, chickpeas ~1.3-2.5 mg / 100 g) and whole grains contain zinc but phytate cuts absorption. Poland everyday: beef, pork, pumpkin seeds, cheese, wholegrain rye — favor animal sources for bioavailability.
- **Prep / recipe:** Soak, sprout, or sourdough-ferment legumes and grains to break down phytate and free up zinc. Toast pumpkin seeds and scatter on skyr, salad, or soup as a daily topping. Pair plant zinc with an animal-protein source in the same meal to lift absorption.
- **Template line:** 30 g pumpkin seeds (~2 mg zinc) OR 150 g beef (~7 mg zinc)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** UL = 40 mg/day (food + supplement). Chronic zinc above the UL (even ~50 mg/day) induces COPPER deficiency — the copper-depletion ceiling: anemia, low white cells (neutropenia), and a myeloneuropathy (numbness, tingling, gait/balance problems) that can be irreversible. STOP and see a clinician on: unexplained anemia, pins-and-needles or balance issues, persistent fatigue on high-dose zinc. Acute high single doses cause nausea (take with food). Never run high-dose zinc long-term without paired copper. Hair loss in Hashimoto's -> check zinc AND ferritin before adding more.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Zinc x copper COMPETE at the intestinal transporter — high zinc depletes copper (add ~1-2 mg copper if supplementing chronically); this is the key ceiling. Zinc + selenium act synergistically for deiodinase T4->T3 conversion and antioxidant defense. Absorption antagonists: phytate (wholegrain, legumes), high-dose calcium, and iron supplements all cut zinc uptake — space them. Coffee/tea reduce absorption. Zinc's own interference with levothyroxine is modest, but any calcium/iron co-formulated with a zinc pill must be kept ~4 h from the levothyroxine dose; take zinc itself in the eating window away from the pill.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Zinc deficiency is relatively common in hypothyroid patients (thyroid hormone is needed to absorb zinc, so under-treatment can drive deficiency, which further blunts T4->T3 — a loop). Small trials (including zinc-deficient cohorts) show zinc, sometimes with selenium, improves thyroid markers when a deficiency is corrected. But an antibody-lowering effect is unproven (see [[Hashimoto and Diet]] — micronutrient -> antibody evidence is weak). So treat zinc as correcting deficiency for T3 conversion, immunity, energy and hair, not as a disease-modifying antibody therapy.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** Food-first covers 11 mg/day easily for a meat-eater (beef + a daily handful of pumpkin seeds + cheese). Supplement 15-25 mg only for documented deficiency, persistent hair loss, or a plant-heavy/high-phytate diet — and if you do, pair copper after ~1 month. Avoid routine high-dose 'immune' zinc as a habit because of the copper ceiling.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** Take zinc supplements on EATING days WITH food — zinc on an empty stomach causes nausea, so never at the fast-break on an empty stomach; put it mid-meal once you've eaten. Food sources (seeds, red meat, cheese) naturally fall inside the eating window, so a meat/seed eater needs no fast-day dosing. On the 24h fast day, no zinc is required; resume on the refeed. Keep zinc (and any paired copper/calcium/iron) away from the levothyroxine dose window.
- **Evidence:** Moderate that zinc is required for deiodinase-driven T4->T3 conversion and that repletion improves thyroid markers in DEFICIENT patients (mechanistic + small/older trials, e.g. a 1994 zinc-deficient cohort and animal data; a 2026 narrative review of iron/zinc/copper in thyroid disease). Strong for the zinc-copper antagonism and the 40 mg copper-depletion ceiling (well-established, case reports of zinc-induced hypocupremia). Antibody-lowering effect: weak/unproven (carried from [[Hashimoto and Diet]]). Generalizability: zinc-thyroid trials are small and often run in deficiency states or mixed/institutional populations — no male-specific dose trials; guidance here rests on the RDA (11 mg men) and mechanism, which fit the male reader.

### 7. Iron / ferritin

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Iron is a cofactor for the heme-dependent enzyme thyroid peroxidase (TPO) that builds thyroid hormone, and for the peripheral T4->T3 conversion; low iron therefore blunts hormone synthesis and worsens fatigue/hair loss. The companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] already flags micronutrients->antibody effects as weak — this item is about symptoms, energy and conversion, NOT about moving TPOAb.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Poland-available heme sources (best absorbed): beef/pork, beef liver 'watrobka' ~6.5 mg/100 g (chicken liver similar), ground beef ~2.7 mg/100 g, blood sausage 'kaszanka'. Non-heme (need vitamin-C pairing): red lentils 'soczewica' ~6.6 mg/cup cooked, cooked spinach ~6 mg/cup, buckwheat 'kasza gryczana', white beans, pumpkin seeds 'pestki dyni', eggs, dark chocolate. Vitamin-C partners grown/sold in PL: red pepper, parsley, sauerkraut 'kiszona kapusta', citrus, kiwi, blackcurrant.
- **Prep / recipe:** Pan-sear or braise liver 3-4 min/side (soak in milk first to mellow); build a lentil stew and finish with a squeeze of lemon or diced red pepper for the vitamin-C boost. Keep coffee and black tea AWAY from iron meals (tannins/polyphenols block non-heme iron — wait ~1 h). Cooking acidic foods in a cast-iron pan adds a little iron.
- **Template line:** 100 g pan-seared beef liver + salad w/ red pepper & lemon (or: 1 cup lentils + parsley + lemon)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Tolerable Upper Intake Level ~45 mg/day elemental iron for adults (GI-distress ceiling). KEY red flag for this reader: do NOT self-supplement iron without a documented low ferritin — men have no monthly losses and unnecessary iron risks overload / masks or worsens haemochromatosis. Symptom red-flags that warrant checking ferritin: diffuse hair shedding, unusual fatigue, restless legs, pica, pallor, breathlessness. If ferritin is high WITH raised CRP, treat it as inflammation, not iron sufficiency. Excess oral iron also causes constipation/nausea and can deplete zinc/copper.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** ENHANCER: vitamin C 25-100 mg taken WITH the non-heme meal (peppers, citrus, sauerkraut). INHIBITORS at the same meal: calcium/dairy, coffee & tea tannins, phytates (whole grains/legumes/soy), and eggs' phosvitin. Cross-nutrient: high-dose supplemental iron competes with zinc and copper. Levothyroxine: iron chelates the pill and cuts its absorption up to ~39% — take levo on an empty stomach and keep iron >=4 h away (e.g. levo AM, iron evening). Selenium/zinc (siblings in this workspace) are unaffected by iron at food levels.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Autoimmune thyroid disease clusters with GI conditions that impair iron absorption — coeliac disease, autoimmune atrophic gastritis and H. pylori — so a low ferritin can be the reason levothyroxine 'isn't working' (impaired T4->T3). Most iron-deficiency-in-thyroid data are in women; a male reader is at lower deficiency risk, but the same malabsorption comorbidities apply, so test rather than assume. Practical Hashimoto-specific rule: the >=4 h levothyroxine spacing, not a daily iron habit.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** FOOD-FIRST for maintenance — heme-rich meals plus vitamin-C-paired plant iron cover an 8 mg/day male requirement easily. Unlike vitamin D (almost always a pill), iron should be supplemented ONLY on a documented low ferritin / iron-deficiency, ideally with clinician oversight and a follow-up ferritin in 8-12 weeks. Blind iron supplementation in a male is the wrong default.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** During the weekly 24 h fast: take nothing iron-related; iron pills need food/vitamin C and cause GI upset on an empty gut, and any capsule with excipients/calories is better on an eating day. Levothyroxine timing is unchanged (empty stomach, before coffee). On the REFEED / eating day: place an iron-rich, vitamin-C-paired meal in the middle of the eating window, away from coffee and dairy, and >=4 h from the morning levothyroxine dose. If a supplement is prescribed, alternate-day dosing on eating days both improves fractional absorption (lower hepcidin) and sidesteps fast days cleanly.
- **Evidence:** Antibody effect: the companion note flags iron/ferritin -> antibodies as weak (no surviving evidence it moves TPOAb) — carry that verbatim. Iron<->thyroid-HORMONE link: moderate — a 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis found iron deficiency associates with modest reductions in T3/T4 and higher odds of thyroid dysfunction; the TPO-is-heme-dependent mechanism is well established (strong mechanistically). Vitamin-C enhancement of non-heme iron: strong. >=4 h levothyroxine-iron spacing: strong/guideline-level (NICE). The ferritin 70-110 optimization targets: weak — functional-medicine blog origin, not guideline or RCT-derived. Generalizability: ferritin-hair-loss and iron-thyroid cohorts are largely female/premenopausal; the male reader is at LOWER deficiency risk and HIGHER overload risk, so lean test-then-treat.

### 8. Vitamin D

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Vitamin D is an immunomodulator, not just a bone nutrient: its receptor (VDR) sits on T cells, dendritic cells and macrophages, where it pushes the Th17->Treg balance toward tolerance. Deficiency is very common in Hashimoto's and inversely tracks with TPO antibodies. The companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] is skeptical that correcting it moves antibodies — this item keeps that caveat but adds the immune rationale and, above all, the near-universal need to supplement at Poland's latitude.
- **Targets / numbers:** Bone-health RDA/AI is only 600 IU/day (adults <=70) / 800 IU (>70) — a floor, not an optimum. Practical maintenance to reach a functional level is 2000-4000 IU/day of D3; Poland's 2023 national guideline recommends 1000-2000 IU/day for adults (up to 4000 for higher body weight/obesity), at minimum October-April and year-round if sun exposure is low. Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) is 4000 IU/day (EFSA 2023 and US NAM) without medical monitoring; higher (e.g. short-course 50,000 IU/week) only to correct documented deficiency under a clinician.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Food is structurally weak. Best PL-available sources are fatty fish: salmon 'losos' ~500-1000 IU/100 g, mackerel 'makrela' ~550 IU/100 g, herring 'sledz' ~170 IU/100 g, sardines ~190 IU/100 g; egg yolk only ~37 IU each; fortified margarine/plant milks add a little. Sun: at Poland's latitude (49-54.5 deg N) skin UVB synthesis is near zero for ~6 winter months, and Polish winter studies show 80-84% of adults deficient — so sun+food cannot realistically reach 40 ng/mL here.
- **Prep / recipe:** Bake or pan-fry mackerel/salmon (skin-on, 12-15 min at 200 C) a couple of times a week for a modest food contribution. For the supplement, take the D3 softgel WITH the fattiest meal of the day — it is fat-soluble and absorbs poorly on an empty stomach. A D3+K2 (MK-7) combined softgel plus adequate dietary magnesium (activation cofactor) is a sensible one-capsule setup.
- **Template line:** D3 2000-4000 IU softgel taken with breakfast eggs (+ 100 g baked mackerel a few times/week)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Ceiling: 4000 IU/day as the unsupervised UL; go higher only with lab follow-up. Vitamin D toxicity is rare but real with chronic megadosing — it manifests as HYPERCALCEMIA: nausea, excessive thirst and urination, constipation, kidney stones, confusion. Red flags to stop and check serum calcium + 25(OH)D: those symptoms, or a lab result drifting well above 60-80 ng/mL. Do not use standalone 50,000 IU weekly beyond a short clinician-directed repletion course.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** Cofactors: MAGNESIUM is required to convert vitamin D to its active form — a magnesium deficit blunts the response (magnesium is item 10 in this workspace and doubles as a fast electrolyte); vitamin K2 (MK-7) helps route the resulting calcium to bone rather than arteries. Take with dietary fat for absorption. IMPORTANT contrast with iron/calcium: vitamin D does NOT bind levothyroxine, so it needs no 4 h spacing from the thyroid pill. Higher body weight sequesters vitamin D, requiring a larger dose to hit the same 25(OH)D.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Deficiency (<20 ng/mL) affects ~43-50% of hypothyroid/Hashimoto's patients and correlates inversely with TPOAb; certain VDR gene polymorphisms associate with autoimmune thyroid disease. Recent (2023-2025) meta-analyses show supplementation lowers TPOAb/TgAb — but the effect concentrates in patients who were deficient, and antibody titre is a SURROGATE (no proven change in disease progression, symptoms, or levothyroxine dose). Unlike iodine, vitamin D shows no U-shaped autoimmune risk at sane doses.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** SUPPLEMENT — this is the clearest 'a pill is warranted' nutrient in the whole set. At Poland's latitude, sun is absent half the year and food tops out at a few hundred IU per fish serving, so diet+sun cannot reach the target. This is the opposite of iron (test-then-treat only) and selenium (food-first). Ideally dose to a measured 25(OH)D rather than blindly, but supplementing is close to universal here.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** A D3 softgel carries negligible calories and will not meaningfully break a metabolic fast — but it absorbs poorly without dietary fat, so the practical rule is to take it on the EATING day with the fattiest meal (and alongside magnesium). No timing conflict with levothyroxine (no chelation), so it does not complicate the fast-day levo dose. On the 24 h fast day simply skip it and take it at refeed; because vitamin D stores in fat, an occasional missed day is irrelevant, and a weekly/monthly bolus scheduled on an eating day is an equally valid pattern.
- **Evidence:** Per-claim flags: (1) 'Poland needs a supplement' = strong (latitude/season data + Polish 2023 guideline). (2) Immunomodulation mechanism (VDR, Treg/Th17 shift) = strong mechanistically / preclinically, but that is biology, not a clinical outcome. (3) Deficiency prevalence + inverse association with TPOAb = moderate (observational). (4) Antibody-LOWERING from supplementation: the companion note flags this as weak (refuted/split in its own fact-check); newer 2023-2025 meta-analyses upgrade it to moderate (reduces TPOAb/TgAb, strongest in deficient patients) — still a surrogate with no hard-outcome proof, so present the tension rather than overselling. Generalizability: Hashimoto's cohorts skew female, but vitamin D biology is not sex-divergent for this purpose, so the male reader can apply it directly.

### 9. Iodine adequacy (without excess)

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Iodine is the raw material for T4/T3, but in Hashimoto's it is a double-edged nutrient: both deficiency and excess raise thyroid autoimmunity (a U-shaped curve). The companion note [[Hashimoto and Diet]] already covers the excess-iodine MECHANISM in depth (oxidative thyrocyte injury, failure to escape the Wolff-Chaikoff effect, more antigenic thyroglobulin) — reference it. This item is purely practical: how to land at ~150 ug/day from ordinary food without kelp or a megadose.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Poland-available everyday sources that reach ~150 ug without any supplement: iodized table salt 'sol jodowana' (mandatory in Poland since 1997, ~2.3 mg iodine/100 g -> ~71 ug per 1/4 tsp); cow's milk ~85 ug per 240 ml; Greek yogurt ~116 ug per 240 ml; cottage cheese 'twarog' ~65 ug/cup; cod 'dorsz' ~63-158 ug per 85 g (variable); eggs ~26 ug each; cheddar ~12 ug/oz. AVOID as sources: kelp, nori/seaweed in quantity, 'sea vegetable' supplements and kelp-based 'thyroid support' products — a single kelp tablet can carry thousands of ug.
- **Prep / recipe:** No special prep — just cook normally with IODIZED table salt and include dairy + eggs daily plus a whitefish meal once or twice a week; that alone lands near 150 ug. Two practical traps: (1) Himalayan/sea/kosher salt is usually NOT iodized, so swapping to 'fancy' salt can quietly drop your iodine; keep iodized table salt for cooking. (2) Do NOT sprinkle kelp granules or seaweed seasoning as a 'health' add-on.
- **Template line:** 1 boiled egg + 200 ml milk or Greek yogurt + normal iodized-salt cooking (add 85 g baked cod once or twice a week)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Synergies & antagonists:** SELENIUM x IODINE is the key protective pair (item 5 in this workspace): adequate selenium shields thyrocytes from iodine-induced oxidative damage, so never push iodine on a selenium-deficient thyroid. Goitrogens (raw crucifers, soy) compete with iodine uptake but are irrelevant at adequate iodine (per companion note). Iron and vitamin-A deficiency impair iodine utilization. Unlike calcium/iron, iodine does NOT chelate levothyroxine, so there is no spacing rule against the thyroid pill — but note that on levothyroxine the body's hormone is supplied exogenously, so extra dietary iodine has little upside.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** This is THE nutrient where Hashimoto's flips normal advice. Susceptible thyrocytes are injured by excess iodine, and ATA/NIH explicitly list autoimmune thyroid disease as a group harmed at intakes safe for everyone else — so the goal is ADEQUACY, not abundance. Being on levothyroxine reduces the upside of extra iodine further (endogenous synthesis is already down-regulated), leaving mostly downside. Poland-specific twist: mandatory iodized salt keeps the population iodine-replete (national median UIC ~100 ug/L), so for this reader the realistic risk is inadvertent EXCESS (seaweed snacks, 'sea vegetable' supplements) rather than deficiency.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** FOOD-FIRST, and explicitly NO iodine supplement. This is the one nutrient in the set where a supplement is a red flag rather than a help. Ordinary iodized-salt cooking + dairy + eggs + occasional whitefish covers 150 ug comfortably. Only a clinician-documented deficiency (rare in iodine-replete Poland) would justify anything beyond food — the default answer is 'get it from the plate, never from kelp'.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** Iodine needs no fast-day timing and does not break or interact with the fast — it comes from ordinary food on eating days, and body stores buffer status across a single 24 h fast, so iodine status is essentially unaffected by the weekly fast. It also does NOT interact with levothyroxine absorption, so there is no spacing rule on fast or normal days. Practical caution: don't 'make up' for a fast day with a seaweed snack or iodine supplement on the refeed — just resume normal iodized-salt meals + dairy + eggs across the eating window.
- **Evidence:** Per-claim flags: (1) Excess-iodine harm in Hashimoto's = strong (ATA public-health statement, U-shaped population data, established mechanism) — carry the companion note's strong flag verbatim. (2) RDA 150 / general UL 1100 = strong (NIH ODS, national guidelines). (3) The specific ~400 ug Hashimoto ceiling = weak/moderate — a conservative practical figure, not a formally established autoimmune-specific UL. (4) U-shaped curve, i.e. deficiency ALSO raises autoimmunity = moderate (observational/epidemiological). (5) Poland iodine-replete = strong (national UIC surveys post-1997). Generalizability: iodine biology applies to the male reader directly; the strongest excess-risk datasets are population studies and pregnant-women cohorts, but ATA lists ALL autoimmune-thyroid patients as susceptible regardless of sex, so the caution transfers.

### 10. B12 + folate + magnesium + omega-3 bundle

*PRACTICE-layer companion to LIFE/KNOWLEDGE/INFO/Hashimoto and Diet. Reader is male, presumably on levothyroxine. Three nutrients kept in one JSON as sub-parts of the bundle; each field carries per-nutrient detail where it varies.*

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** The 'other high-yield' correction lever. B12/folate deficiency is common in hypothyroid/autoimmune-thyroid patients and produces fatigue and macrocytic anemia that mimics or stacks on hypothyroid symptoms; magnesium low-status tracks with anti-thyroglobulin positivity and doubles as the single most important fast-day electrolyte; omega-3 EPA/DHA is the anti-inflammatory dietary lever and oily fish co-delivers selenium, iodine and vitamin D in one food. Note from companion: correcting these does NOT reliably lower antibodies (companion flags B12/iron 🔴 for antibodies) — the justification here is symptoms, energy and fasting comfort, not antibody titres. See companion note 'Micronutrients' + 'Fasting' sections; do not re-argue the antibody mechanism here.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** b12: Animal-only. Poland-available: wątróbka (liver, very dense), beef, eggs, dairy, and the oily fish below (śledź/herring, mackerel, salmon, sardines). ~1 egg = ~0.6 µg; 100 g salmon ≈ 3 µg; 100 g beef liver ≈ 60+ µg.; folate: Leafy greens (spinach, kale), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans), liver, eggs, beetroot, asparagus. 100 g cooked lentils ≈ 180 µg DFE; 100 g spinach ≈ 150 µg.; magnesium: Kasza gryczana (buckwheat — very Polish, ~230 mg/100 g dry), pumpkin seeds (~150 mg/30 g), dark chocolate 70%+ (~65 mg/30 g), almonds, oats, spinach, legumes. Realistic day of food gets a man to ~250-350 mg; the gap is why a supplement is common.; omega3: Oily fish twice weekly: salmon (3 oz/85 g ≈ 1500 mg EPA+DHA), sardines (1 can/92 g ≈ 900-1480 mg), mackerel, śledź/herring (Polish staple). ALA plant sources (walnuts, flax, chia) convert poorly to EPA/DHA and don't replace fish.
- **Prep / recipe:** B12/folate: poach or gently pan-cook eggs and fish rather than hard-frying (very high dry heat degrades some B12); serve liver once a week. Magnesium: cook kasza gryczana as a batch side (soak-free, ~12 min), toast pumpkin seeds to scatter on salads/yogurt. Omega-3: bake salmon 180 °C 12-15 min with lemon; keep canned sardines/mackerel as a no-cook rye-bread lunch. Pair folate-rich legumes with a vitamin-C veg to also help non-heme iron (cross-link item 7).
- **Template line:** 150 g baked salmon + lemon (omega-3 + Se + iodine + B12) · 1 can sardines on rye · 30 g pumpkin seeds + ¾ cup cooked buckwheat (magnesium) · liver or eggs 1x/week (B12/folate)

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** b12_folate: B12 has no toxicity ceiling. Folic acid UL 1000 µg/day synthetic — and high folate can MASK B12-deficiency neurological damage, so correct/confirm B12 before loading folate.; magnesium: Supplemental UL ~350 mg/day elemental (above food) is set on the diarrhea threshold, not toxicity — loose stools = drop the dose or switch citrate→glycinate. Real caution: renal impairment (kidneys can't clear Mg → cardiac/CNS toxicity) — get labs first if kidney function is reduced.; omega3: High doses >3-4 g/day carry a bleeding-time and atrial-fibrillation signal in some trials; fishy reflux at high doses. Keep supplemental EPA+DHA ≤3 g/day without clinician oversight.; red_flags: Persistent fatigue + pallor + tingling/neuropathy → check B12/folate (and pernicious-anemia work-up). Palpitations, muscle cramps or poor sleep during a fast → magnesium/electrolytes, not a reason to push the fast through.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** internal: B12 + folate are co-dependent in one-carbon/homocysteine methylation; folate masks B12 (see safety). Magnesium is a cofactor for vitamin-D activation and for hundreds of enzymes.; with_levothyroxine: Magnesium (as a mineral supplement/antacid) can bind levothyroxine — separate the dose by ~4 h (cross-link item 11). Calcium competes with magnesium for absorption; don't take a big Ca and Mg dose together.; food_web: Omega-3 via oily fish co-delivers selenium (100 g sardines ≈ 48 µg Se, salmon ≈ 41 µg) + iodine (canned sardines ≈ 30-60 µg) + vitamin D — one food advancing items 5, 8 and 9. Depleters to watch: metformin and PPIs lower B12; high alcohol lowers folate and magnesium.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Autoimmune polyendocrine clustering: pernicious anemia (autoimmune atrophic gastritis, anti-parietal-cell / intrinsic-factor antibodies) co-occurs with Hashimoto's above baseline — so a Hashimoto's man with low B12 warrants a pernicious-anemia work-up rather than just oral B12. None of these three nutrients is shown to lower thyroid antibodies (companion: B12 🔴, magnesium-TgAb 🟡 observational, omega-3 thyroid-specific 🟡/🔴); treat them as energy/symptom/anti-inflammatory levers, never as substitutes for levothyroxine.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** b12: Food-first for omnivores. Supplement (oral 500-1000 µg) if documented low, vegetarian/vegan, on metformin or PPI, or >60; intramuscular if pernicious anemia / malabsorption.; folate: Food-first — easily hit from greens + legumes; supplement only on documented deficiency.; magnesium: Realistically supplement-warranted for most: Western diets run short and it doubles as the fast-day electrolyte, so a 300-400 mg glycinate is a reasonable standing item even without a lab flag.; omega3: Food-first via 2 oily-fish meals/week suffices for most. A 1-2 g EPA+DHA capsule only if fish intake is low or you're deliberately chasing an anti-inflammatory dose.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** Magnesium is the star fast-day item: 300-500 mg elemental (glycinate preferred on a fast day — citrate can loosen stool), zero calories, does NOT break the fast and does NOT trigger insulin/interrupt autophagy; take it with sodium (3-5 g) and potassium (2-3 g) to prevent the cramp/palpitation/poor-sleep triad (cross-link item 1). B12 and folate are eating-day items — take B-vitamins with a meal (better tolerated, and food-bound B12 needs stomach acid/food); no need on the fast day. Omega-3: a fish-oil capsule contains fat calories and technically breaks a strict fast, so keep it in the eating window; oily fish itself is an excellent refeed/eating-day protein-and-fat first food (cross-link item 2) — front-load it early in the window.
- **Evidence:** b12_folate_deficiency: 🟡 — deficiency prevalence in hypothyroid/AITD is real but observational and wide-ranging (6-68% across cross-sectional studies, mostly non-Western cohorts, not male-specific). Antibody-lowering claim: 🔴 (companion: no surviving evidence). Correcting for fatigue/anemia: sound clinical practice.; magnesium_tgab: 🟡 — the low-magnesium ↔ TgAb-positive/hypothyroidism link comes from a large cross-sectional study (Wang et al., Sci Rep 2018, ~1257 Chinese adults) — pre-2023, cross-sectional (association not causation), general population not Hashimoto-male. Magnesium-as-electrolyte for fasting comfort: 🟢 mechanistically, weak formal trials.; omega3: 🟡/🔴 — 2024-2025 Hashimoto's work is mechanistic (EPA/DHA-derived resolvins/maresins, CRP correlations) and small, often bundled with a gluten-free co-intervention and female-skewed Polish cohorts; no hard-outcome RCT of EPA/DHA alone on thyroid antibodies. General anti-inflammatory benefit of omega-3 is 🟢, but the thyroid-specific payoff is not established.; generalizability: Reader is male; the magnesium-TgAb and omega-3 Hashimoto's datasets skew non-Western and/or female and small — extrapolate cautiously.

### 11. Levothyroxine spacing + goitrogen/soy handling

*PRACTICE-layer companion to LIFE/KNOWLEDGE/INFO/Hashimoto and Diet ('Levothyroxine timing' + 'Goitrogens' sections). Reader is male, presumably on levothyroxine. This item is about TIMING and food-drug interactions, so nutrient-shaped fields (food_sources, food_first_vs_supplement) are reframed to the binders/goitrogens the dose must be timed around.*

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** Absorption logistics are the single most controllable determinant of whether a given levothyroxine dose actually works. Consistent empty-stomach dosing, spaced away from coffee, calcium, iron, soy and high-fibre, keeps TSH stable without a dose change; goitrogen 'handling' is mostly reassurance — cook crucifers, don't mega-dose raw, and keep soy away from the pill. Do not re-argue the goitrogen mechanism (companion note covers it 🟢, 'overstated at normal intake with adequate iodine'); this is the do-it layer.
- **Lab / biomarker target:** TSH is the primary readout — keep it in the individual target band and watch for creep after any change in dosing routine or food timing; fT4 secondary. Recheck TSH ~6-8 weeks after a deliberate, consistent change to the routine (new timing, new soy/calcium/iron habit). TPOAb/TgAb are NOT moved by dose timing — don't use them to judge spacing.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** The 'binders' to time the dose around, Poland-available: calcium (mleko, twaróg, yellow cheese, fortified plant milks, Ca/Mg supplements); iron (red meat, wątróbka, fortified cereal, iron pills); coffee/espresso; soy (tofu, mleko sojowe, edamame, soy 'protein' isolates in bars/shakes); high-fibre (otręby/bran, psyllium, very large raw salads). Goitrogen foods to cook/cap: raw kapusta (cabbage), jarmuż (kale), brokuł, brukselka (Brussels sprouts), kalafior — all fine cooked.
- **Prep / recipe:** Build a fixed morning ritual: swallow levothyroxine with plain water on waking, start a 45-60 min timer, THEN breakfast and coffee. Push the calcium-, iron- and soy-heavy items into lunch/dinner (away from the dose). Cook crucifers — steam 4-5 min or roast 15-25 min at ~200 °C — which knocks out most goitrogens; enjoy them freely once cooked. Keep raw crucifer juicing/large raw-kale salads occasional. Bedtime alternative: take levothyroxine 3-4 h after dinner, which also sidesteps the whole morning-food clash.
- **Template line:** Levo + water on waking → 45-min timer → breakfast & coffee. Soy / dairy / iron / big salads go in later meals. Crucifers cooked, not raw-by-the-bowl.

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Never double-dose a missed pill into the next day's food window; don't normalise taking it with coffee. Red flags: TSH creeping up or hypothyroid symptoms returning (fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, brain fog) on an unchanged dose → audit timing, coffee, calcium/iron, and new PPI/antacid use before assuming the dose is wrong. Raw-crucifer red flag is only the historical extreme (≈1-1.5 kg RAW cabbage/day plus iodine deficiency → myxedema); ordinary amounts are safe. If TSH is unstable on a soy-rich diet, the fix may be a dose review with the endo, not just tighter spacing.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** This is the interaction core: calcium, iron, coffee, soy and high-fibre each reduce levothyroxine absorption when taken with the dose — space all of them (Ca/Fe ≥4 h, soy ≥3-4 h, coffee ~60 min, fibre out of the 4 h window). PPIs and antacids raise gastric pH and blunt absorption — separate and flag to the endo. Grapefruit and espresso are minor additional dampeners. Vitamin C taken with levothyroxine can slightly IMPROVE absorption in patients with low stomach acid (minor, not a strategy to rely on). Selenium×iodine and other cofactor pairings are handled in items 5/9.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** The goitrogen fear is overstated when iodine is adequate (companion 🟢) — the real Hashimoto's-specific absorption issues are (1) autoimmune atrophic gastritis / low stomach acid and co-occurring celiac, which make absorption erratic, so day-to-day CONSISTENCY matters more than the exact minute; and (2) soy separation, which is worth doing regardless of the unsettled interference debate because it's zero-cost. Bottom line: cook and cap raw crucifers, do NOT restrict cooked crucifers, keep iodine adequate-not-excess.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** Prefer getting calcium and iron from food placed in the eating window rather than from pills that clash with the morning dose. If calcium or iron SUPPLEMENTS are genuinely needed, schedule them ≥4 h from levothyroxine (e.g. evening if the dose is morning). Whole soy foods are fine when separated by ≥3-4 h; be stricter with concentrated soy-protein isolates (shakes/bars) near the dose. This isn't a 'supplement vs food' nutrient decision so much as a scheduling decision — the levo owns its own empty-stomach window.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** A 24 h fast day makes levothyroxine timing EASIER, not harder: the stomach is already empty, so absorption is optimal — just keep black coffee 30-60 min AFTER the dose (cross-link item 1). The one rule that matters across fast and eating days is identical timing on the same clock, because consistency is what stabilises TSH; don't move the dose just because it's a fast day. If you use bedtime dosing, a fast day automatically satisfies the '3-4 h after last meal' condition. Critically: do NOT skip levothyroxine on a fast — it is a medication, contains negligible calories, and does not break the fast.

### 12. Meal templates + Mediterranean pattern + shopping list

*PRACTICE-layer synthesis for LIFE/KNOWLEDGE/INFO/Hashimoto and Diet. Reader is male, presumably on levothyroxine, doing ONE 24 h fast/week. This is the plate-and-shopping-list item that operationalizes items 1-11; it cross-links rather than re-derives their numbers.*

**Why & how much**

- **Role:** The synthesis plate. A whole-food Mediterranean base is the evidence-favored default (companion 🟡: adherents show lower oxidative stress) and it natively supplies selenium, zinc, iodine, B-vitamins, magnesium and omega-3 through fish, olive oil, nuts, legumes and fermented dairy — so most micronutrient items are covered by the pattern rather than a pill shelf. Gluten-free is layered on only for celiac or a symptom/antibody-driven trial. The two-template system (fast-day vs eating-day plate) is what turns all the prior items into something cookable.
- **Lab / biomarker target:** Track the composite protocol, not one number: TSH/fT4 stable across fast cycles (companion's fasting rule), TPOAb/TgAb trend (don't over-index — diet moves these little), plus the optimization panel from the nutrient items — ferritin ~90-110 ng/mL (functional target, above the standard >30 floor), 25(OH)D ~40 ng/mL (functional target), serum B12 (>400-500 optimization), RBC magnesium. Celiac tTG once (drives the Med-vs-GF fork). Functional targets are flagged as optimization aims, not standard cutoffs.

**Food & practice**

- **Food sources:** Concrete whole-food shopping list (Poland-available), grouped:
• PROTEIN: eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, twaróg, salmon, sardines, śledź (herring), mackerel, lentils, chickpeas, tofu (kept away from the levo dose).
• FATS: extra-virgin olive oil, walnuts, almonds, pumpkin seeds, 2 Brazil nuts/day.
• CARBS/FIBRE: kasza gryczana (buckwheat), oats, brown rice, whole-rye bread, quinoa; plentiful vegetables (peppers, tomatoes, spinach, courgette) and berries.
• FERMENTED: natural yogurt, kefir, kiszona kapusta (sauerkraut), kimchi, kombucha.
• MICRONUTRIENT ANCHORS: Brazil nuts (Se), oysters/red meat (Zn), iodized salt + dairy + eggs + whitefish (iodine ~150 µg/day without kelp), oily fish (omega-3 + Se + iodine + D).
• SUPPLEMENT SHELF (the standing exceptions): vitamin D 2000-4000 IU; magnesium 300-500 mg (also the fast-day electrolyte); electrolytes (Na/K/Mg) for fast days.
- **Prep / recipe:** Batch-cook model: kasza gryczana + roasted mixed veg + baked salmon as a repeatable tray. Overnight oats with kefir, walnuts and berries for an easy protein/fibre breakfast. Sardines on rye with olive oil + lemon as a no-cook lunch (omega-3 + Se + iodine + B12). A big salad — chickpeas + peppers + pumpkin seeds + olive oil + lemon — pairs vitamin-C veg with non-heme iron (cross-link item 7). Steam or roast crucifers rather than eating them raw by the bowl (cross-link item 11). Put one fermented side on every eating-day plate.
- **Template line:** Eating-day plate: ½ plate roasted veg + 150 g salmon + ¾ cup buckwheat + olive oil + 2 Brazil nuts + a side of kefir or sauerkraut. || Fast-day: water + electrolytes (Na/K/Mg) + black coffee — no food.

**Safety & interactions**

- **Safety ceiling + red-flags:** Don't over-restrict: blanket gluten-free or AIP without an indication risks nutrient gaps (fibre, B-vitamins, iodine) and disordered-eating drift. Don't chase iodine through kelp/seaweed 'superfoods' — Hashimoto's UL is ~400 µg/day, far below the 1100 µg general ceiling (companion 🟢). Fermented foods: back off if histamine-intolerant (flushing, headache after sauerkraut/kombucha). Red flag: unintended weight loss, worsening fatigue or hair loss on a restrictive plan → loosen the restriction and check ferritin/zinc/thyroid labs rather than tightening further.
- **Synergies & antagonists:** The plate physically encodes the cofactor web: pair vitamin-C veg with non-heme iron (legumes + peppers/lemon); keep selenium adequate BEFORE pushing any iodine (never iodine-load a Se-deficient thyroid, companion + item 5/9); separate the levothyroxine dose from the calcium/iron/soy/coffee/high-fibre-heavy meal (cross-link item 11); fermented foods + fibre feed short-chain-fatty-acid-producing gut bacteria linked to lower inflammatory signaling. Keep coffee out of the levo window. Magnesium and calcium compete — don't mega-dose both together.
- **Hashimoto caveat:** Mediterranean beats gluten-free on the evidence for most NON-celiac Hashimoto's: a 2025 meta-analysis found gluten-free had no significant effect on TSH/fT3/fT4 in non-celiac patients, while Mediterranean adherence lowers oxidative stress (companion). So gluten-free is mandatory only if celiac-positive; a symptom-driven trial is optional, not the default. Keep COOKED crucifers in the rotation freely (goitrogen fear overstated at normal intake with adequate iodine); keep iodine adequate-not-excess. Gut/fermented-food and microbiome links are mechanistically plausible but not proven to change hard thyroid outcomes.
- **Food-first vs supplement:** The Mediterranean base makes most micronutrients food-first — selenium (Brazil nuts, fish, eggs), zinc (red meat, seeds), iodine (dairy, eggs, iodized salt, whitefish), B-vitamins, omega-3 (oily fish), magnesium (buckwheat, seeds). The standing pill exceptions: vitamin D almost always needs a supplement (sun + food are weak, companion), and magnesium is worth supplementing partly because it doubles as the fast-day electrolyte. B12 and iron only if labs show deficiency. So the rule is: food-first plate + a small fixed supplement shelf (D + fast-day Mg/electrolytes), not a broad micronutrient stack.

**Fast fit & evidence**

- **Fast integration:** This two-template system IS the fast integration for the weekly 24 h fast (cap at ONE/week, companion + item 3). Fast day = water + electrolytes (Na 3-5 g / K 2-3 g / Mg 300-500 mg) + black coffee, levothyroxine on the usual clock (cross-link items 1 & 11). Break the fast GENTLY — kefir/yogurt/broth/eggs, protein + fat + fibre before any carbohydrate to avoid a glucose spike into insulin resistance (cross-link item 2) — then move to a normal Mediterranean eating-day plate. Front-load protein and the oily-fish / Se / Zn foods early in the eating window; don't compensate for the fast with a late carb bolus. The shopping list deliberately stocks BOTH the fast-day electrolyte items and the gentle refeed first-foods.
- **Evidence:** Mediterranean pattern: 🟡 observational (lower oxidative stress; 2023-2024 studies show Med reduces oxidative stress and GF reduces antibodies — but small, short 12-week, often female-skewed Turkish/Polish cohorts). GF-only-if-celiac: 🟢 (2025 Nutrients meta-analysis, 3 RCTs, non-celiac GF no TSH/fT3/fT4 effect). Fermented foods / gut-microbiome modulation: 🟡/🔴 — mechanistic and emerging (SCFA, barrier function), no Hashimoto's hard-outcome RCT. Glucose-stable meal ordering: 🟡 (general metabolic evidence, not Hashimoto-specific). Generalizability: diet-intervention Hashimoto's cohorts are small and skew female; male-specific data are thin, but a whole-food Mediterranean pattern is safe to generalize to a male reader.
