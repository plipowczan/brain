Raphael, the GPT Image 2 + Seedance 2 combination is genuinely impressive for high-end product work. The jewelry commercial output in your storyboard shows how far the quality ceiling has moved in just the last 6 months. What I'm watching closely with Seedance 2.0 specifically: the temporal consistency improvements are real. Previous video generation tools struggled to maintain product detail continuity across cuts — the gem facets would subtly shift between frames. Seedance 2 seems to handle this much more reliably, which is what makes it viable for luxury commercial work where brand consistency matters. The workflow optimization you've built here is the key insight though — it's not just "use AI tools" but "sequence them correctly." GPT Image 2 for the storyboard/reference frames, then Seedance 2 for motion. That sequencing takes a lot of trial and error to figure out. For anyone testing this for product photography: the economics become compelling when you factor in revision cycles. Traditional commercial shoots often require a re-shoot for client revisions. With this workflow, a revision is a prompt update. That's the real value — not just cost per image but elimination of re-shoot costs entirely.

→ GPT Image 2 → Seedance 2 pipeline preserves spatial structure from the reference in a way earlier video generation tools simply couldn't handle. What's impressive is the camera control — architectural flythrough has very specific movement requirements (smooth dolly, consistent horizon line, maintained scale) that most AI video tools produce inconsistently. The output here looks like it respects the camera path intention, which is a real leap. For the AEC industry, this changes the economics of early-stage visualization significantly. Pre-design renders traditionally required either a dedicated visualization specialist or expensive 3D rendering services. If a project architect can go from Sketchup model to stakeholder-ready video in a morning, you've compressed what was a week-long process. The critical question I'd want to explore: how does it perform on interior spaces vs. exteriors? The lighting control on interiors is harder to maintain across frames, and that's where most AI video tools still struggle for professional architectural presentation.
